# Characters

POV: **Iyo Ballast**, close third person, throughout. Other voices arrive via the interstitial
paper-trail documents (logs, letters, dispatches) so the book stays single-camera — which is also
the game's camera.

---

## The Column (the expedition)

### Iyo Ballast — protagonist
- **Role**: Linewalker First Class; the Column's recovery specialist and, increasingly, its spine.
- **Age/body**: 29. Wiry, wind-burned, moves like she's always on ice because she always is.
- **Voice**: dry, precise, economical. Counts things — steps, breaths, lamp-posts — a rote from
  line training that long ago became how she holds herself together. The count is the book's
  quietest motif and its last line of horror.
- **Wound**: three years ago her sister **Maren** (then 19) walked. Iyo was on shift that night,
  two kilometers down the line, and recovered a stranger instead — correct triage, by the book.
  She has recovered 41 people since. She has never said Maren's name outdoors.
- **Want / need**: wants Maren back, or failing that, wants the dark to be *fightable*. Needs to
  learn what the book knows: that some things can only be lived beside, not beaten.
- **Why she goes**: she found Elo Weir on the line; Kiln requests her; she refuses (mother, duty)
  — until Elo, in passing, in Maren's exact recorded voice, says *"you counted wrong."*
- **Arc**: recovery-tech → witness. She starts the book pulling people out of the dark by rote and
  ends it able to talk to the dark's people like a diplomat, which costs her the comfort of hating
  it. Final state: keeps a lamp in her window like everyone else, and knows why.
- **Relationships**: mother Odile (furnace hand, estranged-close, the person she can't grieve in
  front of); Brakes (becomes the sibling-shaped friendship); Kiln (father-transference she
  resents, then accepts); Elo (her opposite number: the recovered one who didn't come back).
- **Game notes**: player character. Traversal + recovery mechanics; the count as UI motif.

### Warrant Officer Ude Kiln — the living archive
- **Role**: expedition commander by Compact writ; the last living crew of the Errand.
- **Age/body**: ~85 biological, 260 chronological (the Founders' Sleep — frozen pre-crash, woken
  60 years ago as the last of the archive rotation). Tall ruin of a man, formal, immaculate.
  Dying of cumulative cryo-damage: "the Sleep collects."
- **Voice**: pre-crash formality nobody else has; calls the ship "she"; calls the colonists "the
  passengers" when tired, which unsettles everyone.
- **Wound**: he is the only person alive who remembers the sky of Meridian, and he can feel those
  memories eroding. His nightmare is not death; it's outliving his own witness.
- **Why he goes**: duty (the cores are his cargo), mortality (last act), and the summons — Elo
  Weir asked for him *by rank*, which no living civilian would know to do. He tells no one how
  much that third reason weighs. (Note: this is played as dread, not as a secret to be solved.)
- **Arc**: authority → testimony. His memory fails first and worst in the dark (he "remembers"
  the Errand's descent *from outside her hull*, which is impossible). He gets the whole Column to
  the wreck on discipline and dies aboard, on the command deck, logged home by his ship. Chapter
  22 is his.
- **Game notes**: mission-giver energy early, escort-fragility late; his failing memory can be a
  narrative mechanic (briefings that subtly change).

### Dr. Nef Cistern — physician-priest
- **Role**: Column surgeon; ordained in the Quiet Order; the Order's official eyes on the journey.
- **Age/body**: 47. Soft-spoken, heavy-handed with kindness, hands always busy (rosary = a length
  of knotted heat-main gasket).
- **Voice**: liturgy leaks into everything ("well, the body is a boiler," etc.).
- **Wound**: he has presided over 30 years of families of the Walked with a doctrine that calls
  their people *malfunctions*. He no longer believes it and cannot say so.
- **Arc**: orthodox → undone → *ministering*. Farside breaks his theology open (ch. 19–20): the
  dark is not absence; then what has he been telling the grieving? He walks — voluntarily,
  serenely, leaving a letter that reframes it as a calling: *"someone should say the offices for
  them."* The Column mourns; Farside congratulates. Nothing in Book One says which is right.
- **Game notes**: healer/support; his side content is pastoral (he mends people, then asks them
  to mend him and they can't).

### Ottla "Brakes" Furnace — convict-engineer
- **Role**: crawler master and machine-priest-without-portfolio; debt-labor, sentence carried on
  the Column's books.
- **Age/body**: 34. Broad, burn-scarred forearms, half-deaf right ear from the furnace accident
  that killed two people and got her sentenced (the accident was her supervisor's fault; the
  supervisor had a surname that mattered; hers is Furnace).
- **Voice**: the funny one — shop-floor deadpan, names everything (the crawler is "Kettle," the
  stove is "the bishop"). Comedy that is 100% armor and reads as such by ch. 16.
- **Quirk that matters**: through her deaf ear she *hears the Hum as music* — has since the
  accident. In the deep dark she starts hearing it resolve into something like a **work-song**,
  and (post-Old Say) her hands begin doing small tasks she didn't decide to do — always correct
  tasks, which is worse.
- **Arc**: property → person → *keeper*. Old Say's death (ch. 16) breaks her; Tapper's choice
  (ch. 23) remakes her. Comes home pardoned ("services to the light"), refuses the Order's
  attempt to ordain her, keeps Tapper.
- **Game notes**: crafting/repair systems; her music-hearing is an audio-design gift (the player
  hears her mix).

### Sergeant Adder Loom — security lead
- **Role**: the Column's soldier; Rimwar veteran.
- **Age/body**: 51. Built like the doorway of an old building. Slow, deliberate, unfailingly
  courteous to the young.
- **Wound**: Cinder Row. Under lawful order he cut the heat-main and ~400 people froze in a
  night. He was 28. He has spent 23 years being the safest man anyone knows, one act at a time.
- **Arc**: penance-by-competence → confession → something like absolution from the worst possible
  source (ch. 10: the frozen town's walkers pause on their rounds, and one nods to him). He comes
  out of Cinder Row lighter and *worse* — absolution he can't refuse from a source he can't
  accept. Survives the book; at the end he takes over training the Line Watch, teaching recovery
  instead of violence.
- **Game notes**: combat lead; his ch. 10 is a no-combat quest by design.

### Solene Antenna — listener
- **Role**: radio operator; the Column's thread home.
- **Age/body**: 19, the youngest by a decade. Quick, homesick, ashamed of being homesick.
- **Why she's aboard**: the Verge's best young ear; she can pull signal out of static by
  something adjacent to perfect pitch. Her mother ran Hearthfall's mast before her.
- **Arc**: link → witness of the impossible. Radio is her identity; the dark ruins radio, then
  does something worse than ruin it (her own voice answering her hail, ch. 13; home broadcasts
  arriving *without static* where static is a law of physics, chs. 20, 25). She survives, but the
  thing she loved is now also the thing she fears, and she keeps doing it anyway — the book's
  quietest courage.
- **Game notes**: the radio is a core diegetic interface (see game notes doc); Solene is its
  voice.

### Tapper — ship automaton
- **Role**: mule, scout, and eventually a character in the fullest sense.
- **Body**: quadruped maintenance unit the size of a big dog's ambitions; patient, tireless,
  scuffed to bare metal. Communicates by tapping work-code.
- **History**: recovered from the Vault cellars, one of three still functional on the Verge.
  Property of the Compact. Its service log goes back to the ship — including entries in the weeks
  after the crash that are simply *missing*.
- **Thread**: in the deep dark, Tapper occasionally obeys orders nobody present gave — turning to
  face the night, standing watch at the wrong angle. At the Errand (ch. 23), the ship recalls it:
  *cargo handling unit, return to station.* Given, for the first time in 260 years, a countermand
  it is *permitted to weigh*, Tapper taps NO and walks out with the Column. Do not foreshadow
  this as a twist; foreshadow it as everyone slowly forgetting Tapper is a thing.
- **Game notes**: companion unit; work-code as a light puzzle language the player actually learns.

### Elo Weir — the returner
- **Role**: guide, omen, cargo, question. Lost six years; recovered by Iyo at the cold-line in
  ch. 1; asks for Kiln by rank; the Compact, on Kiln's insistence, sends him with the Column.
- **Presence**: mid-30s forever; cold hands, unhurried, glad of company. Eats a little bread
  "for the smell." Does not sleep; sits the night watch "so the others don't have to."
  Unfailingly, unbearably kind.
- **Speech rules (binding)**: never lies; never answers *why*; speaks mostly as himself, but
  sometimes a sentence arrives in someone else's voice — recorded-exact, source sometimes
  identifiable, never acknowledged by him. He asks questions only of one kind: invitations.
- **Function per part**: I — the uncanny reason to go; II — the wrong thing inside the camp
  everyone slowly stops flinching from; III — increasingly *accurate* (knows the ice, knows the
  way); IV — home. He delivers the Column, asks the formal question once (ch. 23), stays.
- **Final image**: ch. 26 — seen once more at the cold-line, *facing homeward*, waving.
- **Game notes**: the returner dialogue system's flagship (see game notes; players learn his
  speech rules by ear and the learning is the horror).

### "Ghost" — the Cartographer (joins ch. 17)
- **Role**: a stranger who walks out of the dark on the Sea of Static with accurate maps and
  wrong joints, and trades the maps for company.
- **The device (binding)**: no two people see him quite alike — build, age, even which side his
  bag hangs on. The book states each description once, in dialogue, without reconciliation, and
  never remarks again (tone rule 2). He is courteous, funny, and claims — plausibly?! — to be
  from Farside Landing. Farside, when reached, greets him like a regular they're mildly tired of.
- **He is not explained in Book One.** He stays at Farside ("end of my maps"), gives the Column
  true maps of the dark as a parting gift, and belongs to the parked-questions file.
- **Game notes**: party members' barks describe him differently; no player-facing model swap
  gimmicks — keep it in the writing.

### Column support (the named attrition)
- **Old Say** (Saydu Furnace, no relation Brakes jokes, actual distant relation) — senior
  engineer, Brakes' mentor and the man who taught her to name machines. Dies at the Lead (ch.
  16), hours after the water, warm in the tent, mid-sentence about boiler pressure. The Column's
  first grief that has a body.
- **Pell and Corun Dray** — teamster twins, 22, cheerful, indistinguishable until suddenly,
  terribly, distinguishable: Pell is killed at the Provender toll (ch. 11, human violence);
  Corun walks three nights later (ch. 12) and Iyo's recovery fails on the page.
- **Auditor Vell** — the Compact's observer, pencil-neck courage, secretly filing dispatches home
  that become Warden Frame's homing beacon. Defects to Frame at the Anchorage (ch. 14) out of
  genuine principle, hates himself, and spends the rest of the book doing paperwork as care —
  it's Vell who helps Frame read the roster *out* (ch. 23).
- Eight further teamsters/hands, named lightly in ch. 4–5, individualized by trade tics; four
  turn back with honor at the Reachgate (ch. 12, planned rotation), the rest see it through.

## Home (recurring, non-traveling)

- **Odile Ballast** — Iyo's mother; furnace hand; keeps Maren's chair and bowl. Her ch. 25
  question — *"is she happy?"* — is the book's cruelest line and Iyo's final exam.
- **Provost Sere Marlow** — Hearthfall's chief executive; not a villain, a *survivor of
  arithmetic*: when Lantern worsens after the Column leaves, blaming the expedition is simply the
  cheapest available heat. Authorizes Frame's writ. Eats the loss gracefully when the lights come
  back — which is its own small horror about politics.
- **The Provost of Lantern / Compact delegates, Order hierarchs** — sketched as needed (chs. 3,
  24–25).

## Antagonists (human)

- **Warden Halda Frame** — the Compact's enforcer, sent three weeks behind the Column with a
  recall-or-kill writ and a motorized snatch team. Mid-40s, lawful to the bone, personally brave,
  genuinely believes she is saving home from a doomed provocation. Catches the Column at the
  Anchorage (ch. 14); loses a trooper to the dark in the storm (ch. 15); yokes her team to the
  Column in an armed truce; reaches the racks (ch. 22) and watches her writ turn to paper. Her
  instrument of repentance is her own bureaucracy: reading the roster out, escorting the cores
  home, resigning the wardenship to found the Line Watch (with Iyo) — law repurposed as care.
- **Mother Quill** — proprietor of Saltcamp and de facto sovereign of the Provender. Grandmotherly,
  hospitable, keeps caged walkers as "stock" and sells warmth by the hour. Perfectly human,
  perfectly adapted, the book's argument that the Reach's real apex predator arrived on the ship.
  Recurs chs. 8, 11, 24 (the return toll — she *waives* it, which lands like a threat).
- **The dark** is not a character in Book One and must never speak as one. Its agents are people;
  its manner is courtesy; its motives are parked. See `03-factions.md` and `06-deferred.md`.

## Farside Landing (introduced Part IV)

- **Reeve Ansel Stay** — one-armed head of Farside's council-of-sorts. Warm, blunt, allergic to
  being thanked. Fought "wars" she declines to name the other side of.
- **Mirin** — Farside's listener; Solene's counterpart; runs a radio room that receives Hearthfall
  with impossible clarity and finds Solene's alarm about this charming.
- **The Stayers' culture** (see factions doc): returners at dinner tables; walking treated as
  ordination; the rule they give the Column — *"take nothing from the ship she doesn't hand
  you."*
- **Maren Ballast** — found not at Farside but at the wreck (ch. 22): alive, unaged?, mid-task
  among the racks, kind, *busy*. "I can't now — we're almost done counting." She thanks Iyo for
  coming. She is not rescued, because she is not captive. This is the book's center of gravity:
  the person you came for, well, and occupied, and not coming home.
