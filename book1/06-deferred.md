# Deferred Mysteries & Guardrails

**Purpose of this file**: Book One is written with the mystery layer deliberately parked. This is
where the parked things live, so they stay consistent without being mined. When we do the mystery
pass together (a future working session, by explicit decision), we start here.

---

## The policy

Book One runs **no clue economy**. The unknowns are ambient — weather the Column moves through —
not a puzzle the reader is being dealt cards for. Concretely:

- Nobody investigates. The characters endure, witness, and work. There is no scene whose purpose
  is "learn a fact about the phenomenon."
- No answers are exchanged as plot currency. The dark's people never explain; the paper trail
  carries politics and grief, not lore.
- The book's questions are *felt* questions (is Maren still Maren? was Cistern called or lost?)
  — never *trivia* questions (what's in the basement?).
- Book One ends by changing the **relationship**, not the **information**: the light and the dark
  are now in contact. That's the sequel's inheritance.

## Anti-pattern guardrails (the "obviously AI mystery" list — binding)

The failure mode we're steering away from: mystery that telegraphs its own construction. Specific
bans:

1. **No breadcrumb trails.** No object/phrase/symbol recurring with escalating significance so the
   reveal can point back at it. If something recurs (the count, the Hum, lamps), it recurs as
   *motif* — meaning accretes, but it is never a clue that "pays off" by decoding.
2. **No twist symmetry.** Banned: any reveal of the shape "the X from chapter 2 was actually Y all
   along." Reveals (in later books) must arrive at an emotional right-angle — answering a question
   nobody thought to ask, while the asked questions stay partly open.
3. **No final explainer.** Nobody — human, returner, ship, or narrator — monologues the truth.
   Ever. Understanding, when it comes, comes in fragments the characters pay for, and it is
   incomplete and possibly wrong.
4. **No red herrings.** They're the same pattern mirrored (a planted thing that pays off by *not*
   paying off). Texture is allowed to just be texture.
5. **No Chekhov inventory management.** Some guns never fire. The book should be full of vivid
   particulars that mean nothing beyond themselves — that's what makes the meaningful ones
   invisible.
6. **The reader must never feel the setup click.** Test for any beat: if a reasonably genre-aware
   reader could tag it "this will matter later" and be reliably right, cut it or bury it. Our
   foreshadowing budget is spent on *character* outcomes (Cistern will walk; Tapper will choose),
   not on facts.
7. **Wrongness is never italicized.** Stated once, plainly, moved past (tone rule 2). No character
   compiles a list of anomalies. No one says "that's the third time something like this—"
8. **Explanations cost.** When Book Two+ buys an answer, someone pays something real for it, and
   every answer purchased must open a bigger unpaid question.

## Rules of the weird (authorial physics — private, binding, characters never learn these)

The mindfuck stays trustworthy because it is *lawful*. All strange events in Book One must be
derivable from these rules; if a scene needs to break one, the scene changes or the rule sheet is
amended here, deliberately.

1. **Invitation only.** The dark never uses force. It imitates, it waits, it asks. All of its
   agency on camera is mediated through people (the Walked, returners) and through pattern
   (schedules, rounds, counts).
2. **It pools around grief and heat.** Its attention gathers where warmth and mourning are
   concentrated. (This is why the cold-line customs work at all, why the Column — a rolling
   bonfire of both — is so *interesting* to it, and why safe passage home is granted: the cargo
   of grief the Column carries out is, by its accounting, already paid for. Do not state any of
   this in text.)
3. **It is exact about consent.** The recovery rote (lit side, spoken name, left hand, count) is
   a working counter-protocol whose origin nobody remembers. The formal question (ch. 23) is the
   same grammar from the other side. Bargains are kept to the letter.
4. **Discontinuities are small, physical, and post-storm.** Sky-tears are when the bookkeeping
   shows. Each discontinuity is hand-authored, occurs once, is noticed by at most one person,
   and is never acknowledged twice.
5. **Returners never lie, never answer *why*, and ask only invitations.** They deviate from the
   old pattern (love errands) into work errands (Elo → Kiln) as of this winter. The deviation is
   real and new. (Its meaning: parked.)
6. **No free plot service.** The weird never solves a problem for the protagonists without an
   unstated invoice. The two Hearts are a gift; the wave in ch. 26 says gifts have senders.

## The parking lot (open questions — do not mine in Book One)

Logged so we don't accidentally half-answer them. Each is currently load-tested against all four
candidate truths below.

- Why the **Errand** diverted, and why she came down.
- What is being **counted**, and what happens when the counting is done. ("We're almost done.")
- Why the **light is thinning** while instruments disagree with eyes.
- The fate of the **Second Landing** (Year ~40).
- Farside's **wars** — against whom, ended how, why Reeve Stay has one arm.
- Who or what **Ghost** is, and why no two people see him alike.
- Why **Elo** returned on a *work* errand, and why the ship wants its officer logged home.
- **Solene's clean signal** — broadcasts without static where static is physics.
- The **missing weeks** in Tapper's post-crash service log.
- Why the **recovery rote works** — who taught the first linewalker to count.
- The **extra footprint / off-by-one count** — what walks in the Column's line, what follows Iyo
  home. (Note: whatever it is, rule 1 applies. It will only ever ask.)

## Candidate truths (sketches only — none chosen; we decide together, later)

- **A. The Custodian.** Something native under the ice — old, patient, not malevolent — met
  humanity in the crash and has been *learning personhood* from the Walked ever since. It keeps
  the 20,000 alive because it understood, from the Stayers, that they matter. The counting is it
  finishing something it promised. Warmest truth; risk: sentimentality.
- **B. The Other Cargo.** The Errand carried more than colonists — a seed-mind, an uploaded
  population, an instrument the crew never knew about. The crash freed it; it has been completing
  the colony's mission by other means. "The colony never stopped arriving." Cleanest sci-fi
  truth; risk: familiar.
- **C. The Contract.** During the dying descent, someone aboard made a bargain — the sleepers'
  lives against payment on schedule. The Walked are installments. The counting is an
  amortization table nearing zero. Harshest truth; makes Kiln's summons devastating
  retroactively; risk: makes the dark too legible.
- **D. The Ecology.** No mind at all: a heat-and-grief-metabolizing ecosystem doing what reefs
  do, and every appearance of intention is human pattern-matching on an indifferent process.
  Purest horror; risk: unsatisfying at trilogy scale.

Likely final shape: a braid (A's patience + C's exactness, say) — but **whatever we choose must
independently generate**: the counting, the thinning light, the work-errand deviation, the clean
signal, and the off-by-one. That's the acceptance test for any truth we draft.

## Placement note

The book's last line of defense against pattern-smell is structural: Book One is a complete story
— departure, crossing, arrival, price, return — with the mystery load-bearing *nothing*. If every
parked question above were deleted, the book would still stand as an expedition tragedy about
grief and maintenance. That's the bar we hold when refining chapters.
