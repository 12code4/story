# World Bible — Taper

## The world

**Taper** is a tidally locked terrestrial world in close orbit around **the Ember**, a red dwarf.
One hemisphere faces the sun forever (**the Glare** — rock deserts, boiling salt flats, lethal). One
hemisphere has never seen light (**the Night Country** — ice sheets over a buried ocean, mountains,
darkness absolute). Between them runs a band of permanent dusk a few hundred kilometers wide:
**the Verge**.

The Verge is livable only where geothermal vent fields bleed heat through the crust. Its sky is a
perpetual sunset the colonists call **the Long Evening**: the Ember sits swollen on the horizon,
never rising, never setting. Weather runs in from the Glare as hot dry gales, hits the cold air of
the night side, and drops snow that tastes faintly of iron.

The Ember is a flare star. Its storms lash Taper's magnetic field into vast aurora events called
**sky-tears** — hours or days when the whole sky crawls with light, radio drowns in static,
unshielded electronics die, and (this is known but not said in official records) people's
experiences stop quite agreeing with each other afterward. Sky-tears are why nothing flies on
Taper, and one of several reasons the night was left alone.

Two further facts, one public and one quiet:

- Public: the vent fields are **cooling**. Slowly, generationally — but measurably.
- Quiet: for about a decade, the Long Evening has been getting **darker**. Instruments say the
  Ember's output is unchanged. Eyes say the light is thinner every year. Nobody has an
  explanation, so officially nobody has noticed.

## History

### The Errand

The colony vessel **Errand** left the origin world (called only **Meridian** in ship records; the
colonists know almost nothing else — the Vault's history section was burned in the crash) carrying
approximately **20,000 colonists in cryostasis** and a working crew rotation. Her destination was
not Taper. Why she diverted, and why she came down — these are the oldest of the parked questions
(`06-deferred.md`), and Book One does not open them. What the record shows: she entered atmosphere
badly, shed cargo and hull across two thousand kilometers of the night side, and broke her back in
a rift deep in the dark, a place the survivors named **the Keelbreak**.

### The Founders' Walk (Year 0–1)

Roughly 1,400 people were awake or woken for the descent. The ship's remaining systems could not
sustain them. Ship's officers determined the twilight band was survivable and led an exodus:
**the Founders' Walk**, fourteen months on foot out of the night, dragging two of the ship's nine
fusion cores (**the Hearts**) on sledges, burying their dead in the ice at marked stations. It is
the founding trauma and the founding scripture. The route is dotted with **Stations of the Walk** —
cairns and shelters, now shrines.

Not everyone walked. Some hundreds — the injured, the unwilling, dissenters who refused to abandon
the 20,000 sleepers in the hold — stayed at the wreck. The record calls them **the Stayers** and
records their certain death. (Book One will discover otherwise: see Farside Landing.)

### The Verge settlements (Year 1–181)

The Founders reached the vent fields and built. Three towns grew into the three
**hearth-cities**:

- **Hearthfall** — the capital, on the largest vent field, seat of the Compact, home of **Heart
  One**. Terraced fungus-and-hardy-grain farms, the Vault, the Linewalkers' Hall. Pop. ~26,000.
- **Lantern** — second city, powered by **Heart Two**, famous for its glassworks and its bell
  tower. Pop. ~14,000. Heart Two is failing.
- **Marrow** — mining town on a marginal vent line, tough, poor, proud. Geothermal only; no Heart.
  Pop. ~7,000. The Rimwars (see below) were fought over vent rights in Marrow's district.

**The Second Landing (Year ~40):** an expedition sent back toward the wreck for the reserve Hearts.
It reached the edge of the frozen sea and was never heard from again. The Compact's response was
**the Compact of Staying** — a standing doctrine, half law and half liturgy: *we do not go back
into the dark.* It has held for 140 years.

**The Rimwars (Years 158–164):** the shameful modern wars — hearth-cities and out-towns skirmishing
over cooling vent rights. Ended by the Compact's monopoly on heavy arms and by an atrocity nobody
prosecuted: the freezing of the out-town **Cinder Row** when soldiers cut its heat-main. (Sgt.
Adder Loom was one of those soldiers. Chapter 10 goes there.)

**Now (Year 181):** Heart Two failing; the Walked worsening; the light thinning; and in deep
winter, a returner at the cold-line.

## Technology

Scavenged-industrial, lovingly maintained, nothing new for a century. The look is heavy plate,
brass and iron, cable and rivet, wool and hide over synthetic weave from looms that still run.

- **The Hearts**: sealed shipboard fusion cores, human-height cylinders of impossible density.
  They cannot be repaired, only tended. Their operating tone is **the Hum**, and it is the pitch
  of civilization — literally: instruments are tuned to it, the Order chants to it. Heart Two's
  Hum has gone *sour*, and everyone within earshot of Lantern knows it in their teeth.
- **Power and heat**: the Hearts drive the two big grids; vent taps and furnaces do the rest.
  District heat mains are the arteries of every city, and cutting one is the local equivalent of
  a war crime (see: Cinder Row).
- **Transport**: rail-crawlers between cities on the Verge line; cable-lifts on the terraces;
  sledges and boots beyond. **Kettle**, the Column's crawler, is a refitted freight unit — a
  rolling stove the whole expedition lives against. Nothing flies (sky-tears; also taboo).
- **Radio**: the nervous system of the Verge, and a cultural obsession — every settlement has its
  mast and its **listeners**. Long-range traffic threads between relay stations like
  **Lighthouse 9**. In the deep night, radio behaves badly, then worse than badly.
- **Weapons**: bolt-drivers (industrial nailguns grown up), scatterguns, blades, and flame. The
  Compact keeps the few real ship-weapons locked in the Vault. Violence on Taper is close, ugly,
  and mostly about heat.
- **Automatons**: the ship carried maintenance units; a handful survive, mule-sized quadrupeds of
  patient strength. They do not speak; they tap in a work-code every child learns a little of.
  **Tapper** is one. They are property. (This will be questioned.)
- **Cryostasis**: understood as history, not as practice — the tech that carried the 20,000 and
  the crew rotation. The **Founders' Sleep**: a schedule by which a few crew stayed frozen in the
  Vault and were woken one per generation as living archives. **Ude Kiln is the last.** The Sleep
  damages people cumulatively; Kiln is dying of it.

## Culture

- **Naming**: given names are short and old (Iyo, Maren, Nef, Ottla, Sere, Elo). Surnames are
  **duty-names** — the deck or trade of one's Founder line: Ballast, Kiln, Furnace, Loom,
  Cistern, Antenna, Weir, Frame. A person's surname is a rough map of what their family has done
  for six generations. Marrying "across decks" is normal; children take the working parent's deck.
- **The Manifest**: the ship's passenger manifest is the sacred document — proof that everyone
  came from somewhere. Its civic descendant is **the Manifest of the Missing**: the register of
  the Walked. When the Column musters, families come to add names and small tokens; the Column
  carries the book into the dark. It is the story's holiest object and the game's quest log.
- **Light discipline**: doors are latched at "night" (a bell schedule; there is no night), lamps
  are kept in windows *facing the dark* — a custom nobody thinks about and the book will slowly
  make horrible. Children are taught: *if you hear your name from the dark side of the line, you
  answer facing the light.*
- **The cold-line**: the patrolled boundary road along the night edge of the Verge — berms,
  lamp-posts, bells, and the **linewalkers** who walk it. Their job is to intercept the Walked
  and bring them home. It is respected, superstition-soaked, badly paid work. Recovery is done by
  rote: approach from the lit side, speak the walker's name, take the *left* hand, count aloud.
  The count matters. Nobody remembers why. It works. (Mostly.)
- **Debt-labor**: the Compact's penal economy — convicts work off sentence-debt in the furnace
  pens and machine shops. Brakes is debt-labor. It is slavery with bookkeeping, and the book says
  so.
- **Grief customs**: the Walked are not mourned as dead — there is no word for what they are. A
  family with a walker keeps their chair, sets their bowl at festival, and never says their name
  outdoors. This unresolvable grief is the cultural water everyone swims in — and (per the rule
  sheet) it is exactly what the dark drinks.

## The Quiet Order (religion)

The faith that grew out of maintenance discipline. Doctrine in one line: *the world is a machine
God left running, and prayer is upkeep.* Priests are ordained mechanics; the liturgy is work-song
and checklist; the holy of holies in Hearthfall is Heart One's chamber, where the Hum is clearest.
Silence is sacred because under silence you can hear what needs fixing.

- **Orthodoxy**: the dark is *absence* — unmachine, unmade, meaningless. The Walked are a
  sickness. Heart Two's sour Hum is a maintenance failure and a test of diligence.
- **The Undern** (heresy, growing): the dark is not absence but *neighbor* — another household
  whose light we cannot see. They leave offerings at the cold-line; some volunteer to walk.
  Officially deplored, quietly everywhere, especially since the returners.
- Dr. Nef Cistern is orthodox clergy. His arc is the collision of that doctrine with Farside
  Landing. It does not survive the collision; what replaces it walks into the dark smiling.

## Ecology

Native life is chemotrophic, slow, and mostly of the dark — a fact that should feel *backwards*
and doesn't get explained in Book One.

- **Cindermoths**: palm-sized drifting fliers of the Verge, drawn to lamps, harmless, lovely.
  Their swarms are the twilight band's fireflies. When cindermoths avoid a light, linewalkers go
  home early.
- **Feral dogs**: descendants of the colony's dogs. Working lines are bred and beloved; feral
  lines run the Reach. Dogs will not cross the cold-line. Dogs sometimes point at nothing and
  scream. Everyone owns a dog story; nobody tells them after dark-bell.
- **Lantern reefs**: sessile colonial organisms along warm vent seams in the Night Country —
  fields of cold blue-green light, pulsing in slow patterns. Beautiful, edible (barely), and the
  only road-markers in the dark. Farside farms them.
- **The Benthos**: whatever lives in the buried ocean under the night ice. Known from sounds —
  knocks, long slides, a note like a wet bell — and from one surfacing (ch. 16). Thermovores:
  they eat heat. A surfaced benthid drinking the expedition's lamplight is the book's single
  clear creature scene, and it is indifferent, not hostile. The dark's ecology runs on heat the
  way ours runs on light — this is *shown*, never discussed.

## The Walked, the returners, and the dark (as observed)

What the Verge knows, without explanation, is in Book One never contradicted:

- Some people rise, usually in sleep, and walk nightward. Any age, any temperament. Restraint
  fails strangely — they wait, they negotiate, they are gone the moment attention lapses. Sedation
  delays. Recovery on the line works; recovered walkers mostly stay recovered. For three years,
  the numbers have climbed, and relapse has appeared.
- The Walked do not die of cold on the line. They are found warm. Past the line, they are simply
  not found.
- **Returners** (new this winter, three known, then more): lost walkers who come back out of the
  dark, alive, cold to the touch, calm, courteous. They eat little, sleep never, and speak partly
  in the recorded phrases of *other* missing people — a sentence here, a laugh there, a voice you
  know from a dead recording. Each returner makes their way to someone who loved them and asks —
  once, plainly — *come back with me.* Refused, they wait, pleasantly, forever.
- Returners never lie, never force, never answer *why*.
- Around the Walked and the returners, small things go wrong with agreement: two witnesses of the
  same moment part with different memories of it; a count comes out different twice. Post
  sky-tear, this worsens: items return that were lost, footprints appear *inside* a party's own
  trail, conversations are remembered that were never had. It is always deniable. It is never
  acknowledged twice (tone rule 2).

## Gazetteer of the route (home → wreck)

1. **Hearthfall** — capital. The Vault (ship records, the Sleep chambers, Kiln's rooms), the
   Linewalkers' Hall, the terrace farms, Compact chambers, Heart One.
2. **The cold-line** — the lamp-lit boundary road. Iyo's territory. Ch. 1 opens here.
3. **The Gloaming** — the last inhabited belt: out-farms, the final vent taps, the first Station
   of the Walk. Where the Long Evening fails by degrees and lamps become life.
4. **The Grey Reach** — 800 km of twilight-into-dark: the Founders' retreat path in reverse.
   Abandoned first-settlements, cache sites, graves, shrines. Provender country.
   - **Saltcamp** — the Provender's market town in a salt dome: heat traded by the hour,
     everything has a price, courtesy absolute, cruelty ambient.
   - **Lighthouse 9** — the last crewed radio relay, kept eleven years by a man named Anser.
   - **Cinder Row** — the out-town frozen in the Rimwars. Its doors still keep curfew.
   - **The Glass Fields** — the Errand's debris corridor: melted-and-refrozen plains studded
     with hull shards, picked over for six generations, still not empty. Scav gangs; the
     Provender's toll-bridge over the first great rift.
   - **The Reachgate** — the arch the Founders raised where their Walk began, at the cliff edge
     above the frozen sea. Inscription: WE CARRIED FIRE OUT OF THE NIGHT. Beyond it, no shrines.
5. **The Sea of Static** — 1,600 km of frozen ocean. Pressure ridges, open-water leads, the
   under-ice Benthos, total radio failure punctuated by sky-tears. Named by the Founders for what
   their radios did all the way across.
   - **The Anchorage** — the ship's severed cargo tether, a forty-kilometer steel cable lying
     across the ice with intact cargo pods strung along it like beads: a road, a larder, a
     mausoleum of the life the colony never got to live.
   - **The Lead** — the great open-water crack the Column must bridge. Site of the midpoint
     disaster.
6. **The Vein** — a warm vent seam under the ice at the sea's far shore, lit by lantern reefs:
   the road into the Night Country proper, and Farside's lifeline.
7. **Farside Landing** — the Stayers' descendants: a lamplit town in absolute night, terraced on
   the Vein, farming reefs, keeping customs the Verge would call blasphemy — chief among them,
   treating the Walked as ordinary. Home believes them 180 years dead. They know all about home.
8. **The Keelbreak** — the rift. The **Errand** lies broken across it, city-sized, faintly lit
   from within. The ninth Heart still runs. The hold holds the racks: 20,000 sleepers, alive,
   tended — by the Walked. This is where the book has been going.
