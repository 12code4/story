# Factions & Threats

Design rule: every faction wants heat, and every faction's ethics are downstream of how it gets
heat. Human conflict on Taper is thermodynamics with manners.

---

## The Compact

The governing council of the hearth-cities: provosts, guild-masters, the Order's delegate, the
Linewalkers' Hall. Its founding law is **the Compact of Staying** (Year ~41): *we do not go back
into the dark.* Authorizing the Column means voting to break their own scripture, which is why the
vote is ugly and the mandate fragile.

- **What they want**: continuity. Fifty thousand people, two Hearts, one of them dying.
- **In the story**: Part I quest-giver and obstacle; Part II–III turns antagonist-by-committee
  (Lantern worsens, a Walked surge hits Hearthfall the week after departure, Provost Marlow's
  faction reframes the Column as *cause*, and the recall-or-kill writ goes out with Warden
  Frame). Part IV: confounded by success, gracefully shameless about it.
- **Texture**: minutes, writs, seals, rationing schedules. The paper trail's main author.

## The Linewalkers' Hall

Iyo's guild. Keepers of the cold-line: lamps, bells, berms, recovery rote. Institutionally
humble, culturally enormous — every family owes them someone.

- **Doctrine**: recovery, not combat. You cannot fight the dark; you can only out-patient it.
- **In the story**: the book's moral baseline. The Hall's rote (approach lit-side, speak the
  name, take the left hand, count aloud) is the first thing the book teaches and the thing the
  ending quietly rewrites (Farside protocols; some walkers now come back on their own).
- **End state**: Frame + Iyo found **the Line Watch** — recovery retrained at scale, the first
  institution built on relationship with the dark instead of denial of it.

## The Quiet Order (and the Undern)

The maintenance faith (full write-up in `01-world.md`). Politically: the second pillar of the
Compact, keeper of the Hearts, schooler of children, burier of the dead.

- **Orthodoxy's stake in the Column**: Heart Two's sour Hum is doctrine-threatening. If diligence
  can't fix it, either God's machine can fail — or the dark is *doing* something, which is worse,
  because orthodoxy says the dark is nothing.
- **The Undern**: the growing heresy — *the night is a neighbor.* Offerings at the line; the
  occasional volunteer walker; folk practices (lamps in windows facing the dark predate them,
  but they claim it). After the Column returns (ch. 25), the news of the racks vindicates
  neither camp cleanly and splits both — seeded for Book Two.
- **In the story**: Cistern embodies the collision. The Order's delegate rides the Compact vote;
  Undern hands leave offerings on the Column's sledges at departure.

## The Provender

The Grey Reach's caravan network — merchants, salvagers, toll-keepers — under **Mother Quill** at
Saltcamp. Not a cult, not raiders: *a market*, which out here is scarier.

- **Economics**: they sell heat by the hour, salvage by the kilo, passage by the head. Their unit
  of account is the **therm-token** (stamped slugs of hull alloy). "A warm body is fuel" is a
  Provender proverb and they mean it several ways.
- **The stock**: they recover the Walked off the deep Reach — then *sell them back* to Verge
  families at ruinous rates, and keep the unclaimed as debt-labor. Whether they also *let*
  people walk, to farm the trade, is asked in ch. 8 and never answered, which is answer enough.
- **In the story**: chs. 7–8 (Saltcamp, the stock, the party's first ethics fracture), ch. 11
  (the toll-bridge; Pell's death — a haggling accident, gun-close and stupid, the Reach's true
  face), ch. 24 (the return: Quill waives the toll and asks only for "first refusal on whatever
  you didn't bring back" — the book's most sinister sentence spoken by a human).
- **Design note**: the Provender must never be secretly supernatural. They are what people do.

## The Wardens (Frame's column)

The Compact's enforcement arm: nine troopers, two motor-sledges, one writ. Detailed under
Halda Frame in `02-characters.md`.

- **Function by part**: II — dread on the radio (Solene intercepts the writ broadcast; Vell's
  dispatches suspected); III — the confrontation at the Anchorage, the storm, the lost trooper,
  the yoked truce (enemies sharing watch rosters — the book's best pressure-cooker); IV — Frame's
  writ dies at the racks; the Wardens come home as the cores' honor guard.
- **Design note**: Frame is always *almost right*. Every argument she makes should be one a
  reasonable reader might side with. The book has no villain speeches, including hers.

## Farside Landing (the Stayers)

The descendants of those who never left the wreck's shadow: a town of ~900 on the Vein, farming
lantern reefs in absolute night. Believed extinct by home; running, it turns out, on patience,
reef-light, and an accommodation with the dark that home has no category for.

- **Customs that horrify the Column**: returners at dinner tables, casually; walking treated as
  ordination ("he'll do well"); children taught to answer voices from the dark *politely, facing
  it*; no Manifest of the Missing because nobody is missing — "we know where everyone is."
- **What they will not do**: go aboard the ship uninvited; take anything she doesn't hand them;
  explain. Their one rule for the Column is the plot's governor: *"take nothing from the ship
  she doesn't hand you."*
- **The wars Reeve Stay won't name**: parked (Book Two material). One arm, no story offered.
- **Design note**: Farside is not sinister and not saved — it destabilizes by being *fine*. The
  mindfuck of Part IV is cultural: everything home fears is furniture here, and the Column's
  categories (rescue, victim, loss) stop parsing.

## The dark (threat design, not explanation)

The phenomenon is not a faction and has no representatives who explain it. For Book One it is
written entirely through its observed behavior — the full observation list lives in
`01-world.md`; the authorial rule sheet lives in `06-deferred.md`. Summary of what the reader
experiences:

- **The Walked** — outbound: calm, patient, unstoppable-by-force, recoverable by rote (until
  ch. 12, when a recovery fails on the page).
- **Returners** — inbound: courteous, cold, truthful, invitation-bearing. Never hostile. The
  book's face of the dark, and it is a *kind* face, which is the horror.
- **The weather of wrongness** — post-storm discontinuities, disagreeing memories, the extra
  footprint, the count off by one. Small, physical, deniable, never acknowledged twice.
- **The ecology** — cindermoths, dogs, reefs, the Benthos: the dark has a working food web that
  runs on heat. Whatever the phenomenon is, it lives somewhere on that web's apex — implied,
  never stated.
- **At the wreck** — not a lair: a *workplace*. The Walked, generations of them, tending the
  racks. The ship gives the Column two Hearts freely. Safe passage home is granted. Book One's
  closing dread is precisely that the dark has been, by every observable measure, *generous* —
  and nobody knows what the invoice is. (Elo's formal question in ch. 23 is the only visible
  price: it asks; some hesitate; all may refuse; the refusal is accepted. That this is the whole
  apparent cost should keep readers up at night better than any monster could.)

## Threat matrix by part (for pacing)

| Part | Physical | Human | Uncanny |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | cold-line routine, one cold-injury | Compact politics | Elo recovered; thin light; moths avoid lamps |
| II | terrain, caches, rifts | Provender (Quill, toll, the stock); writ revealed | Kiln's slips; Cinder Row's curfew; Elo in camp; failed recovery |
| III | the Sea: ridges, leads, storms, the Benthos | Frame's interception; Vell's defection | sky-tear discontinuities; wrong voices; Ghost; safe-feeling dread |
| IV | the last cold, exhaustion | (largely spent — deliberate) | Farside's normalcy; the Approach's traffic; the racks; the question |

Deliberate shape: human threat peaks in Part III and is *spent* before Part IV, so the ending's
pressure is entirely uncanny and moral. The dark never has to raise its voice.
