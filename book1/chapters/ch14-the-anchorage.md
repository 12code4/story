# Chapter 14 — The Anchorage

**Part III: The Sea of Static** · **Status: treatment** · Weeks fourteen–fifteen. The fallen
cargo tether. **Set-piece chapter**: the cable road, the pods, and Frame.

**Function**: The book's great melancholy set-piece (a road made of the ship's own lifeline,
strung with the colony's unlived life) collides with its thriller thread (the pursuit catches
up). Vell chooses the law. The storm forces enemies under one roof — loading ch. 15.

## Beats

1. **The cable.** Out of the ridge-fields, impossibly: a *road*. The Errand's severed cargo
   tether, shed in the descent, lying across the Sea dead straight for forty kilometers —
   three meters of braided ship-steel, ice-welded, elevated over the chaos on its own
   half-buried pylons of frozen spray. Kiln stops the Column at its foot and gives it a
   name and a history in one line each — "Cargo umbilical three. I rode it down to the
   surface once, when it hung from her side like a well-rope" — and then they climb up and
   *walk on it*: the book's most iconic traversal image, a caravan single-file on a steel
   line above a black sea, lamps spaced along a dead machine's artery, aurora behind.
2. **The pods.** Strung along the cable where they fell: cargo pods, intact, ice-sealed —
   beads on the road. The Column shelters in them by night and reads them by lamp: manifests
   stenciled at every hatch. POD 117: SCHOOLHOUSE FITTINGS, 4 CLASSROOMS; SLATES, 800.
   POD 121: ORCHARD STOCK, DORMANT — 200 apple, 60 cherry (the Column, who have never seen
   a tree, stand a long time at the racked saplings, freeze-dried mid-sleep 181 years).
   POD 129: FESTIVAL GOODS — LANTERNS, PAPER, 2,000. The colony that never happened,
   itemized. Cistern's Office that night is only the manifest read aloud, slowly, as
   found liturgy, and nobody thinks it's strange. This is the book's elegy chapter; spend
   it fully. (Brakes pockets one paper lantern, flat, folded. Track it: it burns in ch. 24
   at Lantern's relighting.)
3. **Overtaken.** Motor-noise on the Sea — a sound none of them has ever heard at distance —
   and lights running fast and shallow out of the west: **Warden Halda Frame**, two
   motor-sledges, nine troopers, five weeks of gap closed by machines and shorter sleep.
   The interception is staged on the cable itself (she's read the terrain like a warden:
   the road is a chokepoint, and she takes its far end at a pod-gap, troopers dismounted,
   nothing drawn — Frame runs arrests like Adder runs camps: procedure as de-escalation).
   Parley at ten paces on three meters of steel: the writ (she carries the paper; Kiln
   notes, courteously, that he's already declined it by mast and burned the copy — "You'll
   have to serve it fresh, Warden"), her offer (full pardons, escorted return, the Compact's
   word — and behind her eyes, visible only in how carefully she doesn't look at Elo, the
   *other* clause: abatement), his counter (the freight ledger, the cores, Lantern's twenty
   years; "arrest me on my own deck, if your writ swims"). Standoff, total. And Iyo,
   watching Frame watch them, has the chapter's key perception: the Warden is not hunting
   them. She is *terrified for home*, and this is what that looks like wearing a uniform.
4. **Vell crosses.** Into the standoff's silence, Auditor Vell — pouch-empty, sleepless
   since Reachgate — walks the ten paces and surrenders himself to the law he's been
   feeding all along. His statement is the book's best bureaucratic tragedy: he confirms
   his dispatches (dates, contents, all of it, aloud, to the Column's faces — the ledger
   of his small treasons kept as scrupulously as his ration tables), submits himself to
   the Warden's authority, and — this is the beat — *turns and finishes the supply
   handover paperwork first*, because the Column's allowance tables have to balance
   regardless of anyone's loyalties. "The arithmetic doesn't care who's right," he says,
   and signs, and crosses. Nobody stops him. Brakes, of all people, nods to him. It's the
   collar-wearer's nod: she alone thinks he's done something honest.
5. **The sky closes it.** Before either column can move — the aurora, which has been
   crawling wrong all day (Solene's instruments pegged for an hour; she's been saying so;
   pursuit-drama outranked her), stands up off the horizon and **tears**: the biggest
   sky-tear in the book, the Sea lit end to end in moving color, both parties' electronics
   dying mid-shout. Doctrine on both sides agrees on exactly one thing: you do not ride
   out a tear in the open. One shelter in range: POD 130 (MANIFEST: HOUSEHOLD GOODS,
   ASSORTED — the colony's furniture). The chapter ends with the Column and the Wardens —
   twenty-one souls — one returner among them — one automaton, ten drawn-but-holstered
   histories —
   sealing themselves into one steel room together as the sky comes apart, Frame and Kiln
   bracing the same hatch, and Elo, courteous, holding the lamp. End on the hatch-wheel
   spinning shut.

## Character tracking

- **Frame**: on-page at last; built in one chapter as the book's best mirror — Kiln with a
  living constituency. Her terror-for-home perceived by Iyo = the truce's foundation laid
  before the storm forces it.
- **Vell**: the defection — principled, self-convicting, paperwork-first. His arc now runs
  through Frame's column to ch. 23's roster-reading.
- **Kiln**: "arrest me on my own deck" — the ship's officer fully surfaced; the Compact now
  formally behind him, in both senses.
- **Brakes**: the folded lantern; the nod to Vell. Her moral ledger is her own and the book
  trusts it.

## The dread beat

Restrained all chapter (the pods are grief, not fear) until the tear stands up — and one
detail as the hatch shuts: the cable under them, which has hummed faintly underfoot all
forty kilometers (stated once, back in beat 1, as old current or wind — deniable), goes
*silent* the moment the sky tears. As if yielding the floor.

## Paper trail (interstitial after this chapter)

**Pod 130's manifest**, stenciled column of household goods (beds, cradles, door-hinges,
2,000 window-lamps — *the lamps were cargo*; no one in the pod reads it aloud) — with, taped
beside the hatch in a warden's field-hand, Frame's overnight watch roster: both columns'
names interleaved on one page for the first time. Alphabetical. No ranks.

## Game notes

The cable-walk is the act's signature traversal (single-file balance-and-wind sequence, pods
as discrete shelter-scenes with readable manifests — every manifest authored, no procedural
filler). The interception: dialogue set-piece with the standoff staged in the traversal
grammar the player just learned (ten paces of cable between speakers). The tear's onset is
the game's one hard weather-timer. Pod 130's hatch shutting = act-3 midpoint checkpoint,
and the save icon should be the hatch-wheel. (The window-lamp manifest line is findable,
unremarked, missable. Forums will do the rest.)

## Open questions

- Frame's abatement clause: how visible here? (Current: never spoken. She carries it the way
  Adder carries Cinder Row — the reader who's read ch. 10 knows what the word costs her.
  Test-readers should feel the gun in the writ without the text drawing it.)
- Does Elo and Frame exchange even one line pre-storm? (Current: one. She, checking the
  shelter roster against warm bodies, reaches him and stops. He says: "I don't count,
  Warden." She writes him down anyway. Keep — it's both their characters and the book's
  whole thesis in five words each.)
- The cable's hum going silent: confirm against rule sheet (it's ambient wrongness, stated
  once — compliant), and never explain whether the cable was ever carrying anything at all.
