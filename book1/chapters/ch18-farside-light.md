# Chapter 18 — Farside Light

**Part III: The Sea of Static** · **Status: expanded treatment** · Week twenty-two. The Vein; the far
shore; first sight of Farside Landing. Part III finale.

**Function**: The act-out: after the Sea's worst, its one gift — warmth rising through ice,
light where no light can be, and the sound that undoes everyone. The Column arrives at the
threshold of the impossible town with Elo, for the first time, walking in front. Wonder and
dread run the chapter in a single braid and the book declines to separate them.

## Beats

1. **The Vein found.** Ghost's route proves out: the ice changes underfoot — the singing
   pitch rising (thinner? no: *warmer*), mist standing in seams, and then the first
   window: clear black ice and below it, impossibly, **light** — the lantern reefs, no
   longer kilometers down but rising along a warm ridge, green-gold, vast, pulsing their
   slow patterns directly beneath their boots. The Column walks a glass floor over a lit
   country. Nobody speaks for a long paragraph. Brakes lies down on the ice — deaf ear
   down, the book knowing what she's listening for — and when she gets up she doesn't
   report and nobody asks. (The reefs pulse; her hands, at her sides, keep time with
   something. Stated once.)
2. **The shore.** The Sea ends the way the Founders' log said it did — "the ice climbs
   ashore" — pressure-rafted plates stacked against a black coast: the Night Country's
   true edge. The last camp on the Sea is struck in a strange mood the book names
   honestly: *reluctance*. The crossing was the worst thing any of them has done and it
   had become, in the way of crossings, comprehensible — allowance, lamp, next camp.
   The land ahead has no tables yet. Kiln, at the shore cairn (Founders' plate, one
   line: HERE THE SEA GAVE US BACK), does his last full command briefing: cores,
   route, the settlement rumor — treating Farside as terrain until terrain proves
   otherwise. Frame's wardens check weapons quietly, and Iyo watches Elo watch them do
   it with the expression of a host watching guests bring umbrellas indoors.
3. **The sound.** Landfall plus two days, working up the Vein's shore-line — reef-light
   now welling through every seam, enough to walk by lamps-down (the first
   naked-eye navigation since the Gloaming; faces lit from *below*; the book notes
   what that does to faces) — the wind shifts, and it carries sound down the Vein:
   metal on metal, rhythmic (a forge? a bell-schedule?), a dog — a *dog*, barking,
   at distance — and then, unmistakable, in snatches, the thing that stops the whole
   yoked column mid-stride: **children.** Playing. The specific pitched shrieking of a
   game with rules, somewhere out there in the absolute dark of the deadest place in
   the world. The Column stands in the reef-light listening to it with lamp-flames the
   only motion. Adder — Cinder Row's veteran, the man who has heard a frozen town keep
   schedule — is the one who has to sit down on the sledge for a moment. Nobody
   comments (tone rule 2; also mercy).
4. **Elo in front.** From that halt onward the marching order changes without an order
   being given: Elo — who has ridden the Column's line since Hearthfall like patient
   freight, guided only when asked — simply *walks to the front and leads.* Unhurried,
   certain, courteous at every junction of the shore-paths (paths now: cut, maintained,
   *swept*), and the Column follows him the way you follow a man in his own street —
   the ch. 10 image returned with the valence flipped. Iyo, at his shoulder, asks the
   linewalker's question because it is finally, professionally hers to ask: "Elo. Are
   you going home?" And Elo — speech rules holding: no lie, no *why* — considers it
   with his whole attention and gives the Part III curtain line: "I was sent to fetch.
   I'm coming back with everyone I was sent for." Beat. Kindly, precisely: "You were
   all so slow. It was rude of the sea to keep you." (He means it as an apology on the
   sea's behalf. That is the horror. He files the *sea* under things with manners.)
5. **The lights.** They come up the last rise of the Vein as the reef-glow crests it
   from the far side, and there it is across a black harbor of ice: **Farside
   Landing.** Terraced on the warm seam, lamp-strung like Saltcamp, like Hearthfall,
   like every window of home — a town, lit, breathing, its bell tolling some schedule
   of its own, its harbor-ice staked with fishing lines down into the reef-country,
   figures moving on the quays. A place. Kept. The Column stands at the rise with
   five months of road on their backs, looking at the thing that cannot exist and
   looks exactly like home, and the book gives the act's last line to the count:
   Iyo, out of habit older than grief, counting the lamps on the far shore — and
   getting the same number twice, which has not happened since Hearthfall, and
   discovering that *this*, of everything, is what makes her cry. End Part III.

## Character tracking

- **Elo**: freight → guide → *host-adjacent*. His curtain line resets his threat-model
  for the reader: the errand was never Kiln alone — "everyone I was sent for" is the
  whole Column, and the book lets that sit unexamined until ch. 23 makes it formal.
- **Iyo**: the double-counted lamps — the Sea gave her back a true number and it
  breaks her more than the false ones did. Track: she counts freely again from here
  (until ch. 26's off-by-one).
- **Adder**: the sit-down. His body has been the book's most reliable instrument;
  the children's sound is the first thing to overload it.
- **Brakes**: the reef-rhythm in her hands — logged by the reader, unshared by her.
  Ch. 19–20 (machine-songs) build directly on this.
- **Kiln**: terrain-briefing as armor; his hands are now bad on cold mornings (the
  Sleep collecting; ch. 21's failing staged in increments from here).
- **Ghost**: walks the last stretch visibly content, like a man delivering neighbors'
  mail. "End of my maps" is next chapter's business.

## The dread beat

The children — wonder and horror occupying one sound with zero daylight between them.
Secondary: the swept paths. (Someone maintains the approach to the impossible town.
Maintenance is the book's word for love; the Night Country has it.)

## Game notes

Act finale, built as a single continuous approach sequence: ice-window reef-light
(lighting tech showcase; lamps-down navigation as a mechanic inversion — the game has
trained light=safety for twenty hours and now asks the player to *dim* to see), the
sound event (children heard positionally long before any figure renders; no subtitle
until the halt beat — let the audio do it raw), Elo assuming point (escort inversion:
the NPC the player has guarded now pathfinds, and following him is the input), and the
harbor reveal as the act-out vista. The lamp-count is a hidden interaction: a player
who idles at the rise gets Iyo counting under her breath, twice, same number. No
achievement. (Never an achievement.)

### Dialogue keys

- **Kiln** (the shore briefing, terrain-armor): "The settlement is rumor until it is
  terrain. We treat it as terrain. Terrain has never once lied to me." — and the reefs
  glow under everyone's boots while he says it.
- **Adder** (after the children's sound, eventually, to no one): "Cinder Row keeps its
  forms." Beat. "This one's keeping *recess*." — the veteran's instrument overloading in
  his own vocabulary.
- **Iyo / Elo** (the curtain exchange, locked from treatment): "Elo. Are you going home?" /
  "I was sent to fetch. I'm coming back with everyone I was sent for." / "…You were all so
  slow. It was rude of the sea to keep you."
- **Ghost** (at the rise, contentedly, the mail-carrier's register): "There she is. Mind
  the bell — they ring it for themselves, not for you. Takes getting used to."
- **Solene** (lamps-down, faces underlit, quietly to Iyo): "I keep waiting for it to feel
  wrong." / **Iyo**: "It will." / (beat) / **Solene**: "It doesn't." — Part III's exit
  wound in six lines.

  readable; the player may add the Column's own line with the chisel (free text, 24
