# Chapter 11 — The Glass Fields

**Part II: The Grey Reach** · **Status: treatment** · Weeks ten–eleven. The Errand's debris
corridor; the Provender toll-bridge; the first death.

**Function**: The Reach's adventure-thriller chapter — terrain, scavengers, a toll, a haggling
gone wrong — and the deliberate lesson under it: the first blood the road takes is entirely,
banally human. Plus the turn at home arrives as a voice on the wind.

## Beats

1. **The Fields.** The descent corridor: where the Errand shed herself across eighty
   kilometers — plains flash-melted and refrozen into black glass, studded with hull ribs
   like standing stones, six generations picked-over and still not empty. Kiln's narration
   here changes register: he stops being quartermaster and becomes *crew* — he can read the
   wreckage sequence ("she lost the field spine here... she was trying to climb") and the
   Column realizes, listening, that for him this is not ruins. It is the ship, still crashing,
   very slowly, for 181 years.
2. **Scav country.** The Fields' residents: scav lines working the glass with saws and
   sledges, Provender-affiliated or Provender-fearing. Etiquette of the picked-over lands:
   claims marked in trail-code (Brakes' glossary earning its keep), water shared at neutral
   cairns, nobody asks what anybody's hauling. One scav crew shadows the Column a full day —
   not menace: *appraisal* — and their headman finally trades news for stove-time: the Reach
   knows about the Column (Saltcamp talks); the Reach approves ("about time someone went for
   the spares"); and the Reach has heard something the Column hasn't: *"Hearthfall's put a
   price on your turning around. There's a warden on the road."* First human confirmation of
   the pursuit.
3. **The writ on the wind.** That night Solene, working the band's edges, catches Hearthfall's
   high-power broadcast in a storm-skip — official traffic, repeated on schedule: the Compact's
   **Writ of Recall and Abatement** for Relief Column One. Marlow's faction has flipped the
   council: Lantern's Heart worsened sharply after departure; a surge of walkers hit
   Hearthfall the same week; the Column is now officially the *cause* — "provocation of the
   conditions" — and Warden Halda Frame is empowered to recall or abate. The Column hears the
   word from ch. 10's road sign applied to themselves. Vell, notably, is the one who explains
   the legal term fully, precisely, and without being asked, and then is quiet all evening.
   (His dispatches home — suspected by the reader, not yet by the Column — have been feeding
   the case against them. The paper trail has been showing this since ch. 8.)
4. **The toll.** The Provender bridge over the first great rift: a genuine engineering marvel
   (salvaged field-spine trusses; Brakes is furious with admiration) held by a toll-clan under
   Quill's letter-of-mark. The toll: fuel, or steel, or *hours* (labor on the span). Quill's
   sealed letter from ch. 8 buys courtesy but not passage — "the Mother's love and the
   Mother's rates are different ledgers." The haggling is going — fine. Tight, professional,
   fine. And then it isn't: a toll-guard's dog and the twins' bravado and a slipped load and
   a bolt-driver that was in a hand it shouldn't have been in, and **Pell Dray dies on the
   bridge deck** — badly, quickly, over nothing. Not malice: *accident with witnesses*, which
   out here is a category with rules. The toll-clan's own horror is real; their remedy is
   Provender to the bone: they waive the toll. The Column crosses for free, hating every
   plank, Pell wrapped and sledge-borne. "A warm body is fuel" has a corollary nobody says:
   a cold one is a *discount*.
5. **The funeral.** First grave the Column digs: glass-field burial, ship-plate marker punched
   by Brakes (her hands very steady; her face not), Adder's rite of the tool (a worker buried
   with a tool — Pell's knife goes wrapped in his hands; *log this: it returns in ch. 17*),
   Cistern's Office read whole. Corun does not speak at the grave. He goes on not speaking.
   The chapter ends on the Column making camp within sight of the Reachgate on the horizon —
   and on Iyo taking the night watch beside Elo, who says, looking at nothing: "He was quick.
   That's rare on this road." A returner's condolence: true, useless, kind. She lets it help.
   That she lets it help is the chapter's last, smallest wrongness.

## Character tracking

- **Pell** dies; **Corun**'s silence begins (ch. 12 pays it). The twins' ch. 5 individuation
  cashes out exactly as feared.
- **Vell**: the writ explained = his conscience surfacing; defection at ch. 14 seeded as
  principle, not cowardice.
- **Kiln**: the Fields strip his quartermaster armor; "she was trying to climb" is the closest
  he comes to grief in public until ch. 21.
- **Brakes**: fury-admiration at the bridge; the marker-punching. Her competence is becoming
  the Column's condolence-language.

## The dread beat

Withheld, rigorously: this chapter's horror is entirely human (the accident, the waived toll,
the writ's vocabulary). The only weird note is Iyo accepting comfort from the cold man — the
Column's adaptation curve, ticking.

## Paper trail (interstitial after this chapter)

**The Writ of Recall and Abatement**, full text as broadcast: whereas-clauses telling home's
whole political turn in bureaucratic voice (Lantern's decline "aggravated," the surge
"consequent," the Column "provocation"), the powers granted Frame, and the schedule line that
lands like a countdown: *"broadcast to repeat at every bell until acknowledged."*

## Game notes

The Fields: traversal region with claim-etiquette systems (trail-code literacy now mechanical),
the bridge as a negotiation scene with a scripted catastrophe (design honesty: the player
cannot save Pell; the game must not pretend otherwise — no reload-bait, no hidden success
path; grief without game-over is the point). The writ broadcast becomes a recurring radio
object: audible at every camp henceforth until ch. 14, same words, every bell. Let it wear.
