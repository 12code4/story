# Chapter 10 — Curfew

**Part II: The Grey Reach** · **Status: treatment** · Week nine. Cinder Row, the town frozen in
the Rimwars. **Side-quest chapter — Adder's.** No combat. The book's centerpiece horror set-piece
and its centerpiece moral scene, and they are the same scene.

**Function**: Pay off Adder's planted wound at full price. Demonstrate the dark's *manner* at
scale (schedule, rehearsal, courtesy) without one violent beat. Give the book its thesis about
guilt: absolution can arrive from a source you can't accept, and that is not relief.

## Beats

1. **The route problem.** Cinder Row sits in the only good pass through the Reach's spine
   ridge; the alternative costs six days they don't have (Vell's tables, for once, on the side
   of the terrible choice). Adder requests, formally, to walk point through the town. Kiln,
   who has read a service file he doesn't mention, grants it. The Column learns the history in
   the worst way — from road signage: the town's name struck through on the old Compact
   marker, and under it, in the Rimwars' official euphemism, *"abated, Y.161."* Abatement:
   the word from Frame's future writ (ch. 11 will use it about *them*). Nobody explains it to
   Tam or the reader yet. The town explains it.
2. **The town.** Cinder Row under permanent frost: an out-town of maybe four hundred, intact
   the way a held breath is intact. Doors shut. Windows glazed with ice as thick as bottle
   glass. And the detail the chapter is named for: at the old bell schedule's marks — the
   Column's own clock confirms it — the town *keeps curfew*: sounds behind the doors settle;
   the frozen streets go quiet in the shape of civic obedience. Between bells, movement:
   figures at their rounds. The dead of Cinder Row — walked, all of them, the night the heat
   died, and never gone anywhere — going about errands. A woman sweeps her stoop of snow that
   drifts back. Two figures meet at a corner every circuit and stand a moment: the length of
   a greeting. A child's silhouette does the same eleven fence-posts, hand out, tick tick
   tick. Rehearsal. Not haunting: *maintenance.* The town is keeping itself.
3. **The walk-through.** Kiln's orders: single file, no lights above trail-lamp, no voices,
   nothing touched. The Column's discipline against the town's discipline — two liturgies
   passing through each other. The figures do not react to them... precisely: they *make
   room*. A sweeping woman pauses her stroke as the Column passes her stoop; the meeting men
   hold their greeting a beat longer; the traffic parts, courteously, the way you'd let a
   funeral by. Cistern, walking with his gasket-rosary moving, does not say the Office aloud;
   his lips do. Elo walks through like a man in his own street. Tapper — this lands hard with
   no explanation — walks the whole town with its head turned left, watching the doors.
4. **Adder.** At the town's heart: the heat-main junction house, the valve station where a
   28-year-old sergeant under lawful order shut four wheels in sequence and made the cold do
   the Compact's work. The wheels are still shut. His confession — to the Column present but
   really to the town — is quiet, complete, and unforgivable by design: the orders, the
   compliance, the fact that he checked the wheels *twice* because he was, has always been,
   thorough. He does not weep. He does the thing the book has taught us is his grammar: he
   takes out his tools, and in the presence of the Column and the town, he *services the
   valves* — cleans, greases, dresses them, leaves them shut (opening them is beyond any
   repair that matters; the main is dead at the source). Penance in his only language:
   maintenance without hope of function.
5. **The nod.** As the Column files out the far side, the town at its rounds — and one figure,
   an old man on a stoop who has watched them pass the whole length of the street (the only
   figure that watches), stands. Looks at Adder. And nods — once, the community nod, the one
   you give a neighbor who has done a hard day's work. The town keeps moving. Adder walks out
   of Cinder Row upright and unmade: absolved by the only jury with standing, in a currency
   he can't refuse and can't spend. Cistern, beside him, says the chapter's last line, to
   himself, in a voice with no doctrine left in it: "They kept the forms. Nobody taught them
   that. They *kept the forms*." End chapter.

## Character tracking

- **Adder**: wound → confession → wrong-source absolution. He is lighter afterward and worse;
  track the change small (he stops volunteering for point; he starts teaching Tam knots).
  His endpoint (ch. 25, teaching recovery not violence) roots here.
- **Cistern**: second load-bearing crack — the dark keeps *forms*. His theology says the dark
  is absence; absence doesn't sweep stoops. Farside will finish what this starts.
- **Kiln**: granted the request; walked it with them; says nothing all chapter. Command as
  witness.
- **Tapper**: the turned head. (Rule sheet: old orders — a maintenance unit reads a town in
  maintenance. Never explained.)

## The dread beat

The entire chapter is one held beat; its sharpest points are the *making room*, the nod, and
the bell-kept quiet. Guardrail: zero jump-scares, zero contact, zero explanation. The town
never notices them more than traffic notices weather.

## Paper trail (interstitial after this chapter)

**Rimwars service record, Sgt. A. Loom, Y.161 — the abatement order**, reproduced in full
bureaucratic deadness (the word *abate* doing its work), with the modern Hall annotation
stapled to it: *"subject volunteered for Column duty. Cleared. — G."* Two documents; the whole
of the Compact's conscience between them.

## Game notes

The no-combat quest, exactly as designed in `05-game-notes.md`: a walking-space with a live
schedule (the curfew bell as a real in-game clock the town obeys), movement rules imposed
(single file, lamp-limits) that the player keeps or breaks — breaking them does *nothing*
visible, ever, which playtests will prove is scarier than any consequence. Adder's valve
service is an interactive rite (the game's slowest, most deliberate input sequence). The nod
is the game's most expensive single animation. Spend it.
