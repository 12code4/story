# Chapter 6 — Gloaming

**Part I: The Verge** · **Status: treatment** · Weeks two–three. The Gloaming: the failing edge
of the Long Evening; the first Station of the Walk.

**Function**: The threshold chapter — walking out of daylight *forever* — and the Founders' story
made physical at the first Station. Part I closes with the first deniable wrongness.

## Beats

1. **Light arithmetic.** The Gloaming rendered as slow loss: each march-day the Ember sits
   lower and redder behind them; noon-bell needs lamps now; colors begin to leave (the book
   tracks which — red goes last; the world turns to rust, then to iron). The psychological
   note, stated plainly once: every person on this Column has lived their whole life inside a
   sunset. They are now leaving the only light any of them has ever known, on foot, at a
   walking pace — slow enough to feel it go. Some of them get quiet. Brakes gets loud. Both
   are the same thing.
2. **Out-farm hospitality.** The last inhabited steading: an Undern family who host the Column
   in a barn warmed by their bodies' own economy of beasts and bread-oven. Night talk:
   the farmer's matter-of-fact theology ("the night's a neighbor; you're going calling; take
   bread") lands very differently out here than it did as city eccentricity. Their daughter
   shows Solene the household's window-lamp — and the family's list, kept behind it, of every
   name the house has lost to the dark in four generations. Every window-lamp in the Verge,
   it turns out, has a list behind it. (The custom nobody thinks about, opened once, quietly.)
3. **The first Station of the Walk.** A Founders' shelter-cairn: dome of ship-plate and stone,
   the interior wall scratched with Year-1 names and tallies — *how many we still are* — the
   number falling from wall to wall. Cistern leads the rite (the Offices of Arrival, read in
   reverse for travelers going the wrong way — a liturgical improvisation that visibly
   unsettles him even as he does it beautifully). Kiln stands at the wall a long time. He knew
   some of these hands. He corrects, from memory, one scratched name's spelling — then stops
   correcting and just stands. First on-page contact with the scale of what he carries.
4. **Craft interlude.** The Column's working rhythm in montage-texture: Brakes and Old Say
   tuning Kettle's boiler for cold it was never rated for; Adder re-rigging loads as hauling
   bodies tire; Iyo teaching the twins the recovery rote by firelight *as a story*, because on
   the line you teach it before you need it (the reader is being retaught too — ch. 12 depends
   on this rote being second nature). Elo listens to the lesson with the expression of a man
   hearing his own language spoken with an accent.
5. **The wrongness.** Last camp of Part I. The cindermoths — which have orbited every lamp of
   every camp since home, the Verge's one unfailing comfort — are absent. Not dead, not
   fled-in-panic: the lamps burn alone, and out at the edge of lamplight, once, a drift of
   moths passes by *around* the camp, keeping a distance, like traffic avoiding an accident.
   Stated once. Not discussed. Dovie stops whistling for a night. Part I ends on the Column's
   lamps burning in a dark that has stopped sending its small ambassadors.

## Character tracking

- **Cistern**: the reversed rite — his first professional act with no doctrinal floor under it.
- **Kiln**: the wall of names; grief with 260 years of compound interest.
- **Iyo**: teaching the rote = the book quietly reloading its central mechanism before firing
  it (ch. 12).
- **The Column**: unit cohesion real now; the barn night is the warmest the book gets until
  Farside — which is the point of both.

## The dread beat

The moths. Rule-of-the-weird compliant (no force, no display — just an ecology politely
recusing itself), and the book's first sign that the Column is now the *noticeable* thing in
the landscape.

## Paper trail (interstitial after this chapter)

**Station One wall rubbing** (Vell takes it, officious, then folds it like a relic): the Year-1
tallies with the falling numbers, and one line of Founder graffiti the Column does not discuss:
*"the ones who stayed had lamps too."*

## Game notes

Region transition: color-grade as mechanic (the palette desaturating by travel segment — make
the player feel red leaving the world). Station One is the codex-done-right space again:
touchable wall names. The moth absence is an ambient-system beat: players trained by hours of
moth-at-lamp comfort will notice the silence without a single UI prompt. Trust them.
