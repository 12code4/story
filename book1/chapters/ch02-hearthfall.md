# Chapter 2 — Hearthfall

**Part I: The Verge** · **Status: treatment** · The following week. Hearthfall.

**Function**: The texture chapter, deliberately low-plot: the city, the culture, Odile, Maren's
absence, and the machinery (civic and religious) beginning to grind around Elo. The reader must
love this place before the book takes them away from it — and must feel what the Walked cost it.

## Beats

1. **The city as body.** Hearthfall from Iyo's walk home off shift: terraces steaming against
   the Long Evening, the heat-mains ticking under the streets, the Hum of Heart One under
   everything — felt in the teeth more than heard, the pitch of *normal*. Bells run the schedule
   there is no sun to run. Doors latch at dark-bell. In every window facing the night: a lamp.
   Nobody thinks about the custom; the book just shows it, three times, in three houses.
2. **Odile.** Supper with her mother: furnace-hand hands, practical talk, the fierce unsaid.
   Maren's chair at the table; her bowl put out at festival; her name not said outdoors — Odile
   says it indoors, once, like spending something. The scene's job: the shape of a family
   arranged around a hole, and the specific texture of Walked-grief — no body, no word for what
   they are, no ritual that closes.
3. **Elo processed.** The Hall and the Compact don't have a category either. He's lodged in the
   infirmary-chapel under Dr. Nef Cistern (first meeting: Cistern's gentleness, his gasket-cord
   rosary, his orthodox patter — "the body is a boiler" — thinning audibly when Elo thanks him
   for a meal he doesn't eat). Elo is cooperative, unhurried, and cold to the touch; he sits the
   nights "so the others don't have to," and the night-nurses find they sleep wonderfully and
   hate it.
4. **Sara Weir.** Elo's wife — remarried, a child now — comes because she must. He is kind,
   remembers everything, asks her nothing. It is the *nothing* that breaks her: "That's not him.
   Elo would have asked about the baby." First public statement of the returner deviation: the
   old stories (there are old stories, half-believed, from the deep Reach) say they come back
   for love. This one came back for an *officer*. The word travels the city like cold water in
   the mains.
5. **The city reacts.** Vignettes, brisk: the Order's pulpit calling for diligence and silence;
   Undern offerings appearing on the line-berms overnight (bread, lamp-oil, a child's mitten);
   Provost Marlow testing which way the wind blows before saying anything at all; and the
   Linewalkers' Hall quietly doubling patrols because recoveries are up again this month —
   eleven, where five years ago four was a bad month. End on Iyo summoned by a runner: the Vault
   wants her. Kiln has asked for the linewalker who brought Elo Weir in — by name.

## Character tracking

- **Iyo/Odile**: the home Iyo will leave; the debt of care she'll leave it with.
- **Cistern**: introduced in his element and already quietly failing his own doctrine.
- **Elo**: the deviation established (work errand, not love errand) — as dread, not as clue.
- **Marlow**: one scene, weathervane nature planted for the Part II turn.

## The dread beat

Sara Weir's line. Also the night-nurses sleeping *well* — comfort from the wrong source, the
book's recurring move.
