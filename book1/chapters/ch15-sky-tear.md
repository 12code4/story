# Chapter 15 — Sky-Tear

**Part III: The Sea of Static** · **Status: expanded treatment** · Thirty-one hours in Pod 130. The storm
chapter: one room, two columns, and the book's full mindfuck register opened.

**Function**: The pressure-cooker chapter — enemies packed in a steel box while the sky burns —
carrying the book's deepest interior dive: each principal's private wrongness rendered on the
page, once, without resolution. The storm ends the pursuit as a pursuit. A trooper is gone.

## Beats

1. **The room.** Twenty-one souls and an automaton in a cargo pod among the colony's furniture: wardens and
   Column sorting themselves by trade instinct (engineers pooling near the stove they've
   built from crates; soldiers of both parties converging on watch-and-inventory like it's
   church). The tear outside renders through the pod's one thick port: color moving on the
   ice, wrong shadows, the static wall on every set replaced by a sound Solene finally names
   for what it is — not noise: *volume*. "The band isn't empty. It's full." The pod's steel
   sings faintly at intervals. People talk quietly, then less. The chapter's structure from
   here: the room's single night broken into private interiors, one per principal, each a
   page or less, each entered from the common scene and returned to it. (Form note: no
   dream-sequences. Every interior happens *awake*, in the room, in real time — misfiled
   perception, not sleep. That's the horror.)
2. **The interiors.** In order, escalating:
   - **Adder** hears, under the storm's skin, a bell — Cinder Row's curfew pattern, exact.
     He checks his watch against it. The bell is keeping *better* time. He sits with his
     back to the hatch the rest of the night, facing the room, like a man keeping curfew
     for it.
   - **Brakes**, deaf ear to the pod wall, gets the Hum — but resolved, for the first time,
     into what her bones have suspected since the lighthouse lens: a **work-song**. Call
     and answer. The call is the Hum. She cannot locate what answers. Her hands, in her
     lap, keep the count of a task she isn't doing. She watches them do it.
   - **Solene** runs the dead board by ritual and gets, in one four-second window, traffic:
     Hearthfall's evening numbers — read by her mother — *from last winter*. She knows the
     broadcast; she was home for it; she can hear, behind her mother's voice, her own
     fourteen-year-old cough. The window closes. She does not log this one. (First unlogged
     entry of her life — track it.)
   - **Cistern**, saying the Offices inward, reaches the versicle where the Hum answers the
     prayer — liturgically, a pause the congregation holds — and something *holds it with
     him*: the pause arrives inhabited. No voice, no words. Just — attendance. His name,
     he will insist to no one ever, was in it. He finishes the Office. His hands don't
     shake until the benediction. (His walk in ch. 20 roots here: he has spent a life
     addressing the dark and this is the first time it has felt like being *heard*.)
   - **Kiln** — the deepest cut, placed last: he is telling Frame, quietly, factually,
     about the descent (she asked; wardens verify), and mid-sentence his account goes
     wrong: he describes the Errand's fall *from outside her hull* — the whole ship seen
     against the ice-shine, dying lights along her flank, the tether tearing free — a
     vantage no crewman had; he was in the Sleep racks for descent; there *was* no such
     vantage. He hears himself say it. He stops. He says, with the precision of a man
     stepping back from a ledge: "Correction. I was frozen for the descent. I have no
     memory of the descent." Frame, warden to the bone, writes both statements down. The
     silence after is the chapter's coldest object. (Rule sheet compliance: this is the
     one interior that touches the parked layer. It is stated once, corrected once,
     never revisited in Book One.)
   - **Iyo**'s interior is the shortest and the reader may miss it: through all of the
     above, at the room's edge, she has been counting the sleepers — a linewalker's habit,
     heads in a shelter. The count will not settle. Not wildly: by one. Up one, down one,
     stable for an hour, up one. She stops counting. That, for Iyo, is the event.
   - **Elo**, throughout: sits where he sat. Lamp-keeper. During the worst hour — when the
     pod's steel sings longest and every interior above is peaking — he quietly turns the
     lamp's wick *up*. Courtesy. The wrongness is that it helps.
3. **The trooper.** The storm's back breaks near dawn-bell. Muster: Frame's count comes up
   short. **Trooper Imma Gale** — twenty-six, the one who'd shared her ration bar with Tam's
   memory by telling the pod about her own brother's walking, years back, the only warden
   who'd talked freely across the aisle — is gone. The hatch-wheel: dogged from inside, as
   they'd left it. (Nobody says the impossible part aloud. The book states the wheel's
   position and moves on — tone rule 2 at maximum load.) Outside: the tear's aftermath —
   the Sea's surface reset, ridges subtly re-lain, both parties' outbound tracks *gone* —
   and one set of prints leaving the pod, barefoot, nightward, walking-pace. The prints
   begin *nine meters from the hatch.* Iyo, professional, is the one who kneels at their
   start-point and reads them for the record: stride normal, no drag, no hesitation. "She
   was already walking when she touched down," she does not say. She logs the stride.
4. **The truce.** Frame's pursuit is over — she knows it standing at the prints' start. Her
   writ cannot arrest what took her trooper; her machines cannot carry enough fuel forward
   *and* home; her authority, out here, has exactly the jurisdiction the dark grants it:
   none. The negotiation with Kiln is brief, standing, and conducted entirely in operational
   terms because the real terms are unbearable: the columns yoke. Wardens' machines run
   scout-and-cache ahead while fuel lasts; Column's craft (ice-reading, recovery-rote,
   Brakes) covers both; command splits clean (Kiln the mission, Frame the security);
   destination: nightward — "I'll see the thing my trooper walked to," is all the
   explanation Frame ever gives, and the book never needs more. Vell, given the yoked
   ledgers to merge, does the merge and then — noticed only by Brakes — enters Gale's name
   in the Column's Manifest of the Missing case with full formality: *I. Gale. Y.181.
   Warden column. Token: ration-bar wrapper.* The two expeditions leave Pod 130 as one
   body. Behind them the pod's hatch stands sealed, the window-lamp manifest inside with
   its two thousand undelivered lamps, and the chapter ends on the yoked column small on
   the reset Sea, under a sky gone innocently black, everyone's shadow thrown by lamps
   *slightly wrong* for one more page — doubled at angles the lamps don't quite justify —
   stated once, in the last line, unremarked. End.

  in her that paper doesn't hold.

## The dread beat

Chapter of them; the designed peak is the prints beginning nine meters out — the book's
single largest overt impossibility, spent here deliberately, once, at midpoint, so the back
half can afford total restraint. (Budget note: nothing bigger than this until the racks.)

## Paper trail (interstitial after this chapter)

**Frame's field report of the storm**, warden-precise: the shelter roster (the alphabetical
page from ch. 14, now with one name struck), her verbatim record of Kiln's two statements
with neither privileged, the prints' measurements co-signed *I. Ballast, Linewalker 1C*,
and the report's final line, the whole chapter in warden prose: *"No misconduct found.
No cause found. Column proceeds nightward. So does this command."*

## Game notes

One-room chapter = the game's chamber piece: a long night scene with free movement among
NPC clusters, each interior triggered by proximity/attention (the player can miss some —
authored to reward a second playthrough, never gated). Audio owns this chapter: the band's
"volume," the bell, the work-song's answer-line, last-winter's broadcast — all mixed under
the room tone, none flagged by UI. Post-storm: the world-state edit (tracks gone, ridges
re-lain) is the discontinuity system's loudest sanctioned use. The nine-meter prints are a
walkable forensic scene with the measuring tools from the recovery kit — mechanics as
witness, no prompt text editorializing.

## Expanded treatment — content pass

**Decisions taken this pass**: interior order locked (Adder → Brakes → Solene → Cistern → Kiln
→ Iyo, with Elo threaded throughout); Iyo's stays last and nearly invisible. Gale gets one

### Scene list

- **S1 — The room settles** (POD 130 · everyone). Trade-instinct sorting: engineers pooling
  at the crate-stove, soldiers of both columns converging on watch-and-inventory. The tear
  through the one port: color moving on the ice. Solene names the band: "It isn't empty.
  It's full." Turn: Gale's story (her brother walked years back; she talks across the aisle
  because silence is how her family lost him) — the pod's one warm human hour.
- **S2 — The interiors** (the same room, the same night · one principal at a time, returning
  each to the common scene). Adder's bell keeping better time than his watch; Brakes' Hum
  resolving into a work-song, her hands keeping a task's count in her lap; Solene's
  four-second window of last winter (her mother's voice; her own fourteen-year-old cough) —
  unlogged, the first unlogged entry of her life; Cistern's inhabited pause in the Office
  (attendance; his name in it, he will insist to no one, ever); Kiln telling Frame the
  descent and describing it *from outside the hull* — catching it, correcting for the
  record; Frame writing both statements down; Iyo counting sleepers at the room's edge — up
  one, down one, stable, up one — and stopping. Elo, throughout: lamp-keeper; during the
  worst hour, turning the wick *up*. It helps. That's the wrongness.
- **S3 — Muster** (dawn-bell, the pod and the ice · everyone). Frame's count short. The
  hatch dogged from inside, as left. The prints: barefoot, nightward, walking-pace,
  beginning nine meters from the hatch. Iyo kneeling at the start-point, professional,
  logging stride. Turn: what nobody says aloud, stated by the wheel's position and moved
  past.
- **S4 — The truce** (the ice · Frame, Kiln, both columns). Pursuit over — she knows it at
  the prints. Terms in operational language because the real terms are unbearable: yoked
  columns, machines scouting while fuel lasts, split command. "I'll see the thing my
  trooper walked to." Turn: Vell entering Gale in the Manifest case with full formality —
  noticed only by Brakes. Exit: one body, nightward, under an innocent sky; the doubled
  shadows in the last line, once.

### Dialogue keys

- **Solene** (naming the band): "The band isn't empty. It's full." — four words that
  reclassify the whole crossing.
- **Gale** (the story, mid-laugh, to both columns): "My brother walked when I was nine. My
  mother stopped talking about him, so I started talking about everything. It's a system." —
  the most alive warden; write her warm, spend her whole.
- **Kiln** (the correction, for the record): "Correction. I was frozen for the descent. I
  have no memory of the descent." / **Frame**, writing: "Noted. Both entries stand." — the
  scene's coldest object is her pen doing its duty.
- **Frame** (the truce): "My writ doesn't swim, and it doesn't walk either. I'll see the
  thing my trooper walked to. After that we'll discuss jurisdiction."
- **Elo** (asked, once, by Gale's corporal, where Gale went — the grammar holding under
  grief): "Nightward." / "Is she all right?" / "She wasn't taken." — no lie, no *why*, and
  the exact size of the available comfort.

### Game content pack

**Locations**: POD 130 interior (the chamber piece: one room, free movement, NPC clusters,
the port with the tear outside), the morning ice (forensic scene: the prints, the reset
ridge-lines, both parties' erased tracks).

**NPCs**: everyone already aboard; no new faces (design law holds). Gale exits the cast
here; her corporal (Sess) inherits her ration-bar habit in later camp barks (unremarked).

**Interior triggers (design)**: each interior fires by proximity + attention (stand near,
listen); all are missable except Kiln's (mandatory — it's plot-load-bearing via Frame's
report); a second playthrough with different positioning yields different interiors. No
UI flags any of them, during or after.

**Ambient events & barks**: the pod's steel singing at intervals; the stove-crate's crackle
