# Chapter 3 — The Vault

**Part I: The Verge** · **Status: treatment** · Days later. The Vault; Compact chambers.

**Function**: Kiln, the history, and the stakes — the ship made real through the one man who
remembers her. The salvage case argued and won by one vote. Iyo refuses, then signs, for the wrong
reason, which is the right reason.

## Beats

1. **Down into the Vault.** Under Hearthfall: the ship-steel doors, the records hall with its
   burned history section (the origin world reduced to freight labels and a water-stained star
   chart), the empty Sleep chambers — eleven caskets, ten long dark, one lid open like a made
   bed. Iyo escorted past all of it to a room where a very old man in an immaculate pre-crash
   uniform is writing by lamplight with the Vault's electric light switched off. "It flattens
   the page," he says. "Paper should have shadows. Sit."
2. **Ude Kiln.** The interview that is really an audit: he has read her recovery log — 41 names
   — and asks about technique, about the count, about what a walker's hand does at the moment
   recovery takes. (No one has ever asked her craft questions. It half-wins her before she
   notices.) He tells her, flatly, what he is: last of the archive rotation, woken sixty years
   ago into a world that had become a story about him, dying now of the Sleep's arithmetic.
   And he tells her what Elo's asking-by-rank means to him — no one living should know to do it.
   He does not say the word *summons*. His hands say it, once, arranging papers that were
   already arranged. (Played as dread, per policy — not as a secret with a solution.)
3. **The case.** Kiln walks her (and the reader) through the freight ledger: nine Hearts. Two
   walked out with the Founders. One runs the ship's residual systems — *ran*, he corrects,
   161 years ago when her telemetry died. Six in reserve racking, hold deck three. Then the
   other ledger: Heart Two's decay curves, Lantern's twenty years, the vent fields' slow cooling
   under everything. The arithmetic of staying is now also fatal; the Compact of Staying has
   simply stopped being the safe option. "We were always going to have to go back," he says.
   "We waited until it was hardest. That is what institutions are for."
4. **The Compact vote.** Chambers scene, compressed and procedural on purpose: Lantern's provost
   begging; Marlow triangulating; the Order's Prior Vestel voting *no* on doctrine and losing
   control of his own delegation (the Undern-leaning younger clergy abstain); the Linewalkers'
   Hall voting yes with a rider — a linewalker goes with the Column. Authorization passes by
   one vote, freighted with conditions, observers (enter: Auditor Vell, briefly, blinking), and
   the unstated understanding that everyone who voted yes will deny it if it fails.
5. **Iyo refuses.** Kiln asks for her by name; the Hall makes it a request, not an order. She
   says no in the Vault corridor, reasons in order: her mother; her section; the plain fact that
   the expedition is a funeral with logistics — she has hauled enough people *out* of the dark
   to know better than to march seventeen *in*. Kiln accepts it without argument, which is its
   own pressure. On her way out, past the infirmary door where Elo sits with his hands in his
   lap, he says — not to her, exactly, in a voice that is suddenly and precisely Maren's, off a
   festival recording Iyo has worn thin with listening — *"you counted wrong."* Not accusing.
   Conversational. The way you'd remind someone of an errand.
6. **She signs.** No scene of decision — cut straight to her name going into the Column's
   roster, her hand steady, the clerk's not. Chapter ends on the roster page itself: a
   fresh-ruled page, the first names on it, hers wet at the bottom.

## Character tracking

- **Kiln**: authority, mortality, formality as a container for enormous grief; the summons
  acknowledged only by his hands.
- **Iyo**: her refusal is sound and her reversal is not — the book is honest that she goes for
  Maren, not for Lantern. Everything later prices this.
- **Vell** and **Prior Vestel** planted. Marlow's yes-with-exits planted for the Part II turn.

## The dread beat

Maren's voice in Elo's mouth, deployed with terrible precision: not a haunting — a *message
routed through the available channel*. And Kiln's arranged papers.

## Paper trail (interstitial after this chapter)

**Compact minutes, Session 4471, redacted excerpt**: the authorization language ("Relief Column
One... at its own hazard...") with the vote tally and, struck through but legible, an amendment
requiring the Column to carry no member "known to the dark" — withdrawn without vote. No note
says why.

## Game notes

The Vault is the lore-space done right: environmental storytelling (the ten dark caskets, the
burned shelf) instead of codex dumps. The vote can be an overheard/optional scene. Elo's line is
the game's first "voice-swap" audio event — establish the tech and the restraint here.
