# Chapter 1 — The Cold-Line

**Part I: The Verge** · **Status: treatment** · Deep winter, Year 181. The cold-line east of
Hearthfall, one full night shift.

**Function**: Teach the world's grammar through one working shift: heat, lamps, the count, the
Walked, and Iyo — before anything happens. Then have the wrong thing happen: someone walking *out*
of the dark.

## Beats

1. **Open on the rote, mid-action.** Iyo, two hours into shift, is already walking a recovered
   sleeper home: an out-farmer named Duna, barefoot in the snow, warm to the touch, pleasant and
   absent. The rote is shown, not explained: approach from the lit side; speak the name; take the
   left hand; count aloud, every step, all the way back across the line. Iyo's counting voice is
   flat and endless and we understand it is load-bearing — for both of them.
2. **The line itself.** Hand-off at Post 9: lamps, bells, the berm, the lamp-log. Texture of the
   trade: wick-hours per shift are up again this year — the lamps burn longer to throw the same
   light. The instruments say the Ember is fine. The log says otherwise, in columns nobody reads
   as an omen because it's just the log. Duna's family meets them; the thanks are the
   embarrassed, enormous thanks of people who know the odds. Nobody says the word *walked*
   outdoors.
3. **Iyo alone on the long leg.** The quiet stretch of the shift — her father's old section. Her
   private geography: the third lamp-post where she recovered her first; the culvert where her
   father went into the crevasse (mundane death, established plainly); the bend past which, three
   years ago, Maren went, while Iyo was two kilometers east doing her job correctly. None of this
   is flashback — it's a woman walking past furniture that hurts. Cindermoths orbit the lamps.
   She counts lamp-posts without noticing she's doing it.
4. **The wrong direction.** Near end of shift, the bell at Post 11 — a *hesitant* ring, the
   ringer unsure what code applies. Down the line, in the grey where the lamps give out, a figure
   — walking **toward** the light. Steady, unhurried. In 181 years the line has never had traffic
   inbound. The rote has no page for this. Iyo goes out anyway, lit side, name unknown, and the
   figure stops at the correct distance like a man observing a courtesy, and waits for her.
5. **Elo Weir.** Cold hands, calm face, six years gone — she pulls the name up from the Manifest
   of the Missing lists all linewalkers memorize. He is glad to be found. He answers what he's
   asked, except *why*. He asks for — and this lands like a hand on the neck — **Warrant Officer
   Kiln**, by rank. The chapter ends on the walk back to Hearthfall: Iyo, by rote, counting; Elo,
   beside her, gently keeping her pace; and after a while, matching her count under his breath —
   half a step ahead of her.

## Character tracking

- **Iyo**: established as competent, contained, ritual-bound; grief shown as geography, not
  monologue. Her flinch at Elo's counting is the first crack in the containment.
- **Elo**: the book's dread instrument tuned in his first scene — courteous, truthful, wrong.
  Speech rules operative from minute one.

## The dread beat

Inbound traffic. And the counting — hers as armor, his as *fluency*. He knows the protocol from
the other side.

## Paper trail (interstitial after this chapter)

**Linewalkers' Hall — lamp-log, Post 9, excerpted**: five winters of wick-hour columns, climbing;
a margin note in a dead hand from Year 176: *"lamps old? or evening long?"* — never followed up.

## Game notes

Tutorial shift: movement, lamp interaction, the recovery rote as the signature verb (rhythm
mechanic taught here at safe pace). Elo's arrival is the vertical-slice scene: one figure, one
light gradient, no music until his count joins hers.
