# Chapter 8 — Saltcamp

**Part II: The Grey Reach** · **Status: treatment** · Week seven. Saltcamp: the Provender's
market town in the salt dome.

**Function**: The Reach's human civilization, and the book's first true ethics fracture. Mother
Quill. Heat as open commerce. The stock. The Column discovers that the road's worst thing so far
is a *reasonable* thing.

## Beats

1. **Arrival by courtesy.** Saltcamp announces itself as hospitality: outriders meet the Column
   a day out with hot stones and an invoice for the hot stones. The dome itself: a salt cavern
   city, lamp-strung, warm-pocketed, running on traded heat — pipe-fees by the hour, a public
   thermometer wall as the market board, everyone's breath visible except in the paid zones.
   The courtesy is total and load-bearing: in Saltcamp violence is bad accounting, and manners
   are how you can tell everything is for sale.
2. **Mother Quill.** Audience over supper (the supper is excellent; the invoice, presented
   after with dessert, is itemized to the gram). Quill: grandmother-shaped, shawled, delighted
   by Kiln ("an antique! and still walking!"), fluent in everything — Compact politics, vent
   decay curves, the Column's manifest, which she has somehow already read. She offers real
   value: route intelligence for the far Reach, fuel at merely extortionate rates, and one
   piece of free advice, the chapter's thesis, delivered like a recipe tip: "Out here, dear,
   nobody's wicked. Everybody's *warm or not warm*. Start your sums there and you'll never be
   surprised."
3. **The stock.** Behind the market tiers: the pens. Recovered walkers — a dozen, adults and
   two adolescents, docile, warm, waiting — held for "reclamation fees": families in the Verge
   pay ruinous rates for recovery-and-return; the unclaimed work debt in the warm-pockets,
   indefinitely. It is the linewalkers' sacred act run as a business, and the horror is that
   the business *works*: Quill's recovery teams save more walkers per season than Iyo's whole
   Hall. Iyo goes in with the rote to check their condition — and the stock respond to the
   count like line-recovered walkers, docile, fine — except one, an older man, who at the
   count's end pats her hand and says, courteous as Elo: "You're taking too long. They can
   hear the difference now." Stated once. Not discussed.
4. **The fracture.** The Column's argument, in the rented warm-pocket, everyone's breath
   visible at the edges of the paid heat (the room itself doing the chapter's theme): Adder
   wants the pens opened, cost be damned; Cistern wants the two adolescents bought free
   (allowance says one); Vell tables the allowance; Kiln rules mission-first — they buy free
   *one* adolescent (the younger, a Marrow boy named Tam, walked Year 180) and log the rest
   for the Compact "to address" (everyone hears the euphemism). Brakes, who has been debt-labor
   since the accident, says the chapter's quietest line while retuning her collar's fit: "You
   get used to the arithmetic. That's the bad part." Iyo pays Tam's fee half from her own kit
   allotment — and Quill, watching, *discounts it*, pleased, which spoils it, which she knows.
5. **Departure with strings.** Tam folded into the teamsters (he'll rotate home at Reachgate —
   his recovery is one of the book's few uncomplicated mercies). Quill's parting gifts: a
   corrected route sketch, a sealed letter "for the bridge" (the ch. 11 toll — she is sending
   them into her own toll with a courtesy note: the trap as favor), and to Elo — whom she has
   watched all visit with the only unperforming expression she owns — a small bow. Not
   mockery. *Professional respect.* End on the Column walking out of the warmth by descending
   price-tier, the paid heat thinning behind them zone by zone, until the door and the true
   cold, which is at least free.

## Character tracking

- **Iyo**: first collision between her sacred act and its market double; the old man's line
  ("they can hear the difference now") files itself next to Elo's "you counted wrong" — the
  craft itself is being audited from the other side.
- **Brakes**: the collar line — her arc's floor stated in her own register.
- **Kiln**: mission-first ruling — correct, costly, and the first time the Column's respect for
  him takes damage it doesn't recover until ch. 21.
- **Quill**: established; recurs chs. 11 (by letter), 24 (in person, worse).
- **Tam**: small mercy, Reachgate rotation, one door-knock of pure relief in ch. 25.

## The dread beat

The stock responding to the count *better* than line-walkers — the market has optimized the
rote. And the old man's audit.

## Paper trail (interstitial after this chapter)

**Saltcamp invoice, itemized** (supper, stones, pipe-fees, "reclamation: 1 juvenile, discounted,
courtesy of the house") — with Vell's appended objection memo, and under it, in Vell's smallest
hand, a private line he files anyway: *"the discount was the cruelest thing I have seen on this
road."*

## Game notes

Saltcamp = act-2 hub: market systems (heat as literal currency here — spend warmth to stay),
side-quest board via Quill's "commissions," the pens as a designed moral space (the game should
let the player spend real resources freeing stock, and should not balance it to be affordable).
The count-audit line is an earned-dread bark inside the recovery minigame: the rhythm window
subtly widens in Saltcamp — the stock make it *easy* — and one playtester in ten will notice
and feel sick, which is the correct hit rate.
