# Chapter 9 — Lighthouse 9

**Part II: The Grey Reach** · **Status: expanded treatment** · Week eight. The last crewed radio relay.
**Side-quest chapter** (detour off the retreat path, taken for the mast).

**Function**: The book's first pure side quest: one location, one stranger, one fix, one ask.
Loneliness as its own weather. Brakes' chapter as much as Anser's. Restraint horror — the
chapter where almost nothing is wrong, which is what's wrong.

## Beats

1. **The detour.** Solene's professional pilgrimage: Relay Nine is the far end of the thread
   she's held her whole career from the other side — the last crewed station, eleven years kept
   by one man after the Compact stopped funding a second. Kiln allows two days: the Column
   needs the mast (their last high-power window home before the Sea), and the keeper is owed
   the courtesy of every passing column, of which there have been, per the guest book, three.
   In eleven years.
2. **Anser.** The keeper: precise, courteous, talking with the slight over-clarity of a man
   who rations his own voice. The lighthouse itself is kept like a liturgy — brass polished,
   logs immaculate, the great rotating lens (light *and* dish in one Founders-era assembly)
   grinding on a worn bearing he hasn't been able to machine alone. His pleasures are
   inventoried and terrible: the weather, the logs, "the traffic" (he means the birdless sky's
   static), and the window-lamp he keeps — facing the dark, with, behind it, a list of one
   name. (Asked, he says only: "My relief. He walked. I stayed on.") Eleven years of keeping
   a light for a man who went the other way.
3. **The fix.** Brakes and Old Say vs. the lens bearing — the book's fullest engineering
   sequence, competence as intimacy: Anser assisting with the suppressed joy of a man watching
   his church repaired; Brakes, half-deaf ear to the mechanism, tuning the rebuilt rotation by
   feel and *humming the interval* — and getting it, per Old Say's gauge, exact. (Her
   music-hearing established as craft asset before the dark makes it something else. No
   comment from anyone but Old Say's grunt, which from him is a parade.)
4. **The night watch.** Solene relieves Anser for one night — his first full sleep in years —
   and works the great mast: traffic home (the Column's status, families' messages queued
   from ch. 4), the Reach's thin chatter, Saltcamp's price-wire. And one thing more, logged
   because she logs everything: at the bottom of the band, briefly, a signal she describes in
   the log with a nineteen-year-old's precision and no adjectives: *"carrier present, no
   modulation. Bearing nightward. Very old equipment, or very good."* She does not wake
   anyone. It isn't — quite — anything. (Rule sheet compliant: deniable, unrepeated, never
   discussed. Flag: this is the chapter's only touch of the weird and it must stay this
   small.)
5. **The ask.** Payment refused for the bearing, of course; the actual transaction happens at
   parting. Anser gives the Column his eleven years of logs for the Compact's archive ("they
   should know the Reach still reports") — and one envelope: his **relief application**,
   eleven years out of date, addressed to the Hall. "No hurry," he says, in the voice of a man
   who has thought about the sentence for a decade. "But if someone were coming, I'd want the
   paperwork in order." The Column leaves with the light grinding sweetly behind them, its
   beam swinging out over the Reach toward the dark — and Iyo, looking back, counts the
   rotations without deciding to. End chapter.

## Character tracking

- **Brakes**: craft, the hum, Old Say's approval — the chapter banks the warmth that ch. 16
  will spend.
- **Solene**: her first solo watch, her first log entry that costs something to write down.
- **Anser**: one-chapter character built to be carried home as paper — the application recurs
  ch. 13 (silence behind them), ch. 24 (the return visit: light burning, Anser present,
  cheerful, *does not remember writing it*, asks them to tear it up — "someone has to keep the
  light; I'm wanted here"), ch. 25 (Iyo files it at the Hall anyway, with a note).

## The dread beat

Deliberately almost none — the chapter *is* the beat: a man, a light, a list of one name, and
eleven years of discipline against an audience of static. The carrier-with-no-modulation is
the single grace note, buried in a log.

## Paper trail (interstitial after this chapter)

**Relay Nine keeper's log, three excerpted pages across eleven years**: Year 171 (the relief
walked; Anser's first solo entry, procedural, shattering); Year 176 (a sky-tear night: "the

## Game notes

Flagship side quest, one map: lighthouse interior/exterior, the bearing repair as the game's
best crafting set-piece (multi-stage, companion-assisted, audio-led — the player tunes by
Brakes' hum), Solene's night watch as a playable radio sequence (traffic-menu with one
unlisted signal at the band's bottom edge — findable, loggable, never referenced by any system
again). Reward structure: the logs (lore), the application (a quest item that changes meaning
three times across the game), and the beam itself visible on the horizon for the next two
regions — a lit thing behind you, until ch. 13.

### Dialogue keys

- **Anser** (rationed voice): "Mind the second stair. I've meant to fix it since the year I
  meant to fix it."
- **Anser** (the list of one): "My relief. He walked. I stayed on." — complete; nothing else
  is ever offered; nothing else is needed.
- **Brakes** (tuning): "Hear that? No — *there.* When she stops complaining and starts

### Game content pack

**Locations**: the tower (base, keeper's house, spiral stair, lamp room, gallery), the mast
shed (radio set-piece), the sea-facing gallery (the beam interactive).

**NPCs**: Anser (recurs ch. 24 changed — cheerful, no memory of the application, "I'm wanted
here"; recurs ch. 26 as a logged line only). Keeper Bole (the relief who walked, Y171) exists
as the list-of-one and as a possible rack-census face in ch. 22 (game-only, unremarked).

**Side quests / optional content**:
- *The Bearing* — the flagship crafting set-piece (multi-stage: strip, cast a bushing from
  salvage, seat, tune-by-hum). The tune stage is audio-led: match Brakes' hum, not a gauge
  needle. Completing it perfectly (rare) earns Old Say's second grunt — tracked, referenced
  once in his ch. 16 tent scene ("you were there for the lens; mind the spans the same way").
- *The Night Watch* — playable radio sequence: send every queued family message (each one a
  found-text miniature — write 8–10 of them); the unlisted carrier findable at the band's
  bottom edge, loggable, never referenced by any system again.
- *The Guest Book* — read the three entries (Y168 a Provender survey team; Y171 the relief's
  arrival entry in Bole's hand — his handwriting stops mid-signature; Y176 a scav crew,
  two words: "still lit"). Sign it or don't; Anser checks, either way, from the gallery.
- *The Second Stair* — fix it. One minute of trivial carpentry. Anser stands in the doorway
  not-watching with his hands behind his back. The game's cheapest, most effective kindness.

**Ambient events & barks**: the lens's grind → sweetness (before/after audio state the whole
location shares); the beam's pass over camp (light sweeping the player's shadow long, every
40 seconds, all visit — metronome of the place); Elo and Anser's mutual professional ease
(barks only: two lonely trades recognizing each other).

**Items, tokens & documents**: the eleven years of logs (archive crate — paper trail source),
the relief application (quest item with three meaning-states: carried → ch. 24 disavowed →
ch. 25 filed anyway), the bushing (crafted), guest-book page.

**Audio priorities**: the grind-to-true transition (the chapter's emotional spine in one
sound); the beam's mechanical breath; the radio room at night (band wide open — the last
clean signal-scape before the Sea eats everything); Anser's voice warming by measurable
degrees across the visit — direct the VO that way.
