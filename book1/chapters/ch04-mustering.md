# Chapter 4 — Mustering

**Part I: The Verge** · **Status: expanded treatment** · Three weeks of preparation, compressed.
Hearthfall: yards, shops, chapel, mast, Vault cellars.

**Function**: Assemble the Column one person at a time, each met **in their element** so their
competence is established before their company is. Introduce the Manifest of the Missing as the
book's holy object. The chapter's engine is lists — what you pack to leave the light — played for
warmth until the last beat, where it goes cold.

## Beats

1. **Brakes.** The debt-pen machine shop: Ottla Furnace mid-argument with a foreman, rebuilding
   Kettle's boiler train with a debt-collar on her wrist and a cigarette she isn't allowed
   behind her ear. She names every machine (the crawler is "Kettle," the yard-crane "his
   lordship"); she is taking the posting because completion buys her sentence down, and says so
   without shame. Old Say works beside her, mostly silent, entirely watched — everything she
   does that matters, she checks against his hands first. Her deaf ear introduced as comedy
   (she turns her good ear to people she likes; the foreman gets the deaf one).
2. **Adder.** The armory yard: Sergeant Loom drilling teamsters in *not* fighting — load-bearing
   walks, cold drills, rope discipline. His one line of philosophy, to the twins Pell and Corun,
   who are showing off: "Out there the enemy is arithmetic. You cannot punch it." He signs kit
   ledgers all scene; his violence is present only as how carefully he does everything.
3. **Cistern and the chapel.** The infirmary-chapel packing scene: which drugs, which scriptures
   — he weighs both by the gram, allowance-bound. The Order's send-off politics (Prior Vestel's
   blessing that is also a warning); Cistern's private minute with Heart One's Hum, forehead to
   the chamber rail, like a man filling a flask for a desert.
4. **Solene, Tapper, Vell.** The mast: Solene Antenna talking to four stations at once, her
   mother's old headset around her neck, nineteen and trying to make her voice heavier than it
   is; she shows Iyo the Column's radio plan like a student showing homework. The Vault cellars:
   Tapper unshrouded — patient, scuffed, tapping a greeting-code at Kiln that Kiln, to everyone's
   surprise, taps back (the only two aboard who share a first language). Vell arrives with a
   crate of forms and the observer's writ; the Column's first collective act is uniting in
   mild contempt of him — cheap bonding the book will make them all pay for later.
5. **The Manifest of the Missing.** The Hall opens its doors, and the families come. The scene
   the chapter exists for: a queue through the yard, each family adding a name to the book the
   Column will carry, each with a token (a mitten, a whetstone, a festival ribbon, a tooth).
   The mother of a walked son — her name is Weda; her son walked in Year 179 — grips Iyo's
   sleeve, and her ask is unbearably modest: *"If you see him. Just — how he's keeping."* The
   Manifest goes into a wax-sealed case built into Kettle's flank like a reliquary, because
   that's what it is. Odile does not come to the yard. At home, that night, she gives Iyo
   Maren's festival ribbon and says her name indoors, twice, and that is the whole scene.
6. **Cold close.** Final muster in the yard: seventeen souls, one automaton, one crawler, five
   months of supply read out as a litany by Vell (the lists gone liturgical). Elo stands where
   the seventeenth should and everyone has stopped flinching, which Iyo notices, and notices
   herself not flinching, which is worse. Undern offerings appear overnight on the sledges —
   bread, oil, mittens. Nobody removes them.

## Character tracking

- Whole Column now on stage with competencies proven. Brakes/Old Say and Iyo/Brakes bonds
  seeded; Adder's doctrine stated; Solene's youth exposed; Tapper's language established (and
  Kiln humanized by speaking it).
- **Weda** planted for ch. 22/25 (her son will be found at the racks; her door is a ch. 25 stop).

## The dread beat

Small and structural: the Column has already absorbed a returner into its muster line, three
weeks in. Adaptation is the horror. Also the offerings nobody removes.

## Paper trail (interstitial after this chapter)

**The Manifest of the Missing, first page** (as carried): the form's columns — name, year
walked, kin, token — and the first six entries in six hands, the last entry in Iyo's: *Maren
Ballast. Y178. Sister. Ribbon.* (The book's first time seeing Iyo write it.)

## Game notes

The mustering is quest-design gold: each recruitment beat is a small errand in that character's
space (fetch/fix/carry), teaching their mechanic. The Manifest queue is a cutscene worth full
production — it is the game's quest log being *filled by hands*. Ship out with visible tokens
on Kettle's flank.

## Expanded treatment — content pass

**Decisions taken this pass**: the three individualized Manifest entries locked as Corvo Garden
(Weda's son), Pesh Cistern (Duna's cousin, no relation to the doctor — decks are wide), and

### Scene list

- **S1 — The debt-pen shop** (machine yards · Iyo, Brakes, Old Say, foreman Crane). Kettle's
  boiler train mid-rebuild; the collar; the deaf-ear comedy. Turn: Old Say's silence functioning
  as authority. Exit: Brakes signing the posting with her sentence-debt arithmetic said aloud,
  shameless.
- **S2 — The armory yard** (drill ground · Adder, Pell, Corun, teamsters, Iyo observing).
  Not-fighting drills. Turn: "Out there the enemy is arithmetic. You cannot punch it."
- **S3 — The infirmary-chapel packs** (Order house · Cistern, Vestel). Grams of drugs vs. grams
  of scripture; Vestel's blessing-as-warning. Turn: Cistern's forehead to Heart One's chamber
  rail — a man filling a flask for a desert.
- **S4 — The mast and the cellars** (radio mast; Vault cellars · Solene; Tapper, Kiln, Brakes).
  Solene's four-station juggling act and her mother's old headset. Tapper unshrouded; Kiln
  answering its greeting-code. Turn: the only two aboard who share a first language.
- **S5 — Vell arrives** (muster yard · Vell, everyone). Crate of forms; the observer's writ;
  the Column's first collective act is mild contempt. (The book will make them pay for it.)
- **S6 — The Manifest queue** (Hall yard · the families). The chapter's heart: names, tokens,
  the wax-sealed case on Kettle's flank. Turns: Weda Garden's modest ask; Edda Wick entering
  her father with the brass whistle; Iyo's own entry made at home instead — Odile, the ribbon,
  Maren's name twice indoors.
- **S7 — Final muster** (yard, dark-bell · all seventeen, Tapper, Kettle). Vell's litany of
  supplies gone liturgical. Elo standing where the seventeenth should. Undern offerings on the
  sledges by morning; nobody removes them.

### Dialogue keys

- **Brakes** (signing): "Every klick walked is a day off the collar. I'd walk to the sun if the
  ice held." / **Old Say**, not looking up: "Ice won't. Pack the long augers."
- **Adder** (to the twins): "The enemy is arithmetic. You cannot punch it." / **Pell**: "Can we
  charm it, Sergeant?" / **Adder**, considering this seriously: "No."
- **Weda Garden**: "If you see him. Just — how he's keeping. Whether he eats well. He never ate
  well here." — the ask the whole endgame answers.
- **Edda Wick** (entering Corm): "Da rang bells thirty years. If something out there keeps
  time, he'll have found it." — said as a joke; files itself away for ch. 23.
- **Vell** (the litany): "Wick, four hundred hours. Meal, nine hundredweight. Souls, seventeen.
  Signed." Beat. "I will require everything back that is not consumed." — the room laughs; he
  isn't joking; by ch. 23 the reader learns what a mercy his ledgers are.

### Game content pack

**Locations**: debt-pen machine shop (Kettle on the lift), armory yard, infirmary-chapel,
the mast platform, Vault cellars, the Hall yard (Manifest queue staging).

**NPCs**: foreman Crane (one-scene antagonist-in-miniature), Weda Garden, Edda Wick (recurs
chs. 25–26 as a Line Watch trainee), the teamster corps individualized to two tics each
(Dovie's tuneless whistle, Harrow's cards, Nock's knots, Sung's cooking pride, Marta's
steadiness, Ferro's homesickness — plus the Dray twins' patter act).

**Side quests / optional content** (each recruitment beat is a playable errand teaching a verb):
- *Boiler Train* (Brakes): fetch-and-fit a relief valve under her instruction — teaches repair
  interaction and her call-and-answer cadence.
- *Cold Drill* (Adder): pass his rope-and-load walk — teaches encumbrance and pace mechanics.
- *Grams* (Cistern): help him choose the chapel's travel kit — a weighing minigame where every
  added vial removes a hymnal page; the choice UI is the theology.
- *Four Stations* (Solene): hold one of her channels during a traffic surge — teaches the radio
  interface at safe stakes.
- *The Greeting Code* (Tapper): learn tap-code basics — QUERY, AFFIRM, NO, NAME — the literacy
  the endgame's gentlest scene (ch. 22's hand-hold relay) and hardest scene (ch. 23's NO)
  both depend on.
- *The Queue* (Manifest): staff the table with Guyot for one bell — the player physically
  enters three names (Corvo Garden, Pesh Cistern, Corm Wick) and takes custody of tokens.
  This is the quest log being filled by hands, as designed.

**Ambient events & barks**: the yard's rumor economy (three overheard wrongness-free theories
about Elo, all wrong); children chalking the counting-game on the Hall wall; the Undern
offerings appearing overnight (repeatable ambient from ch. 2, escalated).

**Items, tokens & documents**: the Manifest case (wax-sealed, mounted on Kettle), tokens
(whetstone, bobbin, brass whistle, ribbon — each modeled; tokens are the game's most
load-bearing props), Vell's allowance table (paper trail), Maren's ribbon (given at home —
worn on Iyo's wrist from now to ch. 23, then doubled).

**Audio priorities**: the shop's percussion vs. the chapel's Hum-choir; Tapper's first taps
(woody, patient — cast its "voice" here); the queue's shuffle and low voices — record the
crowd as individuals, not walla; the litany read flat and landing huge.
