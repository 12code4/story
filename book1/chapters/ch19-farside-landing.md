# Chapter 19 — Farside Landing

**Part IV: Night Country** · **Status: expanded treatment** · Week twenty-three. The town.

**Function**: First contact with the Stayers' descendants — hospitality that heals the body
while it dismantles every category the Column brought. The horror of Part IV begins here as
pure *cultural dissonance*: everything home fears is furniture, and the hosts are kind, and
nothing is wrong, and that is what's wrong.

## Beats

1. **The quays.** Arrival as anticlimax, deliberately: no gates, no challenge, no awe.
   A quay-crew securing reef-lines looks up at eleven strangers and a returner and an
   automaton walking out of 180 years of silence, and the eldest says — the town's
   register established in its first spoken line — "You'll be the sister's lot. Kettles
   are on." (*The sister's lot.* Iyo hears it. The book does not stop on it; ch. 22
   pays it.) They are billeted, bathed, fed — reef-broth, dense bread, real warmth
   pooled in stone floors off the Vein — with the practiced unfussiness of a town that
   hosts rarely but knows exactly how. Ghost is greeted by name they don't say twice,
   like a regular they're mildly tired of; he takes a corner seat and is home.
2. **Reeve Ansel Stay.** The town's head: one-armed, sixty, warm and blunt, allergic to
   being thanked. The formalities with Kiln are the chapter's spine scene — ship's
   officer meets the Stayers' descendant across a table, 181 years of two divergent
   histories negotiating which of them is the ghost story. Her people's founding line,
   delivered without heat: "Yours walked out and told the world we died. Ours stayed
   and told no one you lived. Even-handed, I'd call it." The practical matters settle
   fast (rest, resupply, guides to the Keelbreak) and then she gives the Column the
   town's one law for the errand, the plot's governor, flat as a safety briefing:
   **"Take nothing from the ship she doesn't hand you."** Asked (by Vell, of course)
   what happens otherwise, she looks at him the way you look at a man who asks what
   happens if you breathe water, and answers: "You'd be taking."
3. **The furniture of the impossible.** The Column at large in the town, and the book's
   quietest sustained horror sequence — nothing occurs; everything lands: returners at
   dinner tables, in work-crews, minding children (cold hands, unhurried, beloved; the
   town's word for them is simply *the returned*, said like a shift designation);
   a woman mending nets beside what is evidently her walked husband, who does not eat,
   and their conversation is domestic and easy; children playing the chalk-tally game
   on the quay-posts — the game is *taught by the returned*, and its rules involve
   turns of being It that end when you're "counted back." The Column watches home's
   entire architecture of grief — the unsaid names, the kept chairs, the lamps against
   the dark — standing here fully inverted: no Manifest of the Missing exists in
   Farside because, as a quay-woman tells Solene with genuine puzzlement at the
   question, **"nobody's missing. We know where everyone is."**
4. **The scan.** Threaded through all of it, Iyo's engine at full rev: she is checking
   every face. Every returner, every shadowed doorway, every work-crew on the quays —
   the book does it without stating it, in the grammar of where her attention goes —
   and Maren is not here. She asks, finally, at the reef-line sheds, using the town's
   own vocabulary because she has already learned it (*adaptation, ticking*): does the
   town know where Maren Ballast is. The answer comes easy as a road direction:
   "Ship-crew, that one. Up at the Keelbreak. Good hands, they say." — and Iyo, who
   has crossed the world on the theory that her sister was *taken*, stands in a
   net-shed absorbing the Night Country's org chart. Ship-crew. Her sister has a
   *posting*. Brakes finds her there an hour later, sits, says nothing, and — the
   ch. 16 debt repaid in kind — is simply present. It's also to Brakes, here, that Iyo
   finally says out loud why she signed (the ch. 3 open question closed): "I came to
   take her home." And Brakes, who buries her dead and services their bridges, gives
   the Part IV thesis back gently: "Sure. And if home's a thing she has two of?"
5. **The wrong comfort.** Close on the Column's first Farside night: fed, warm, safe —
   objectively the best night since Hearthfall — and the camp-discipline instincts
   misfiring in the calm (Adder walking a perimeter that doesn't need him; Solene's
   dead board hissing gentle *local* traffic — Farside has radio, town-band, weather
   and work rosters, utterly mundane, and she lies awake listening to normalcy like a
   forbidden broadcast). Frame, sleepless, finds Iyo at the billet door and the two
   professionals share the chapter's last exchange, looking out at the lamplit quays:
   "Warden's question, Ballast. If nothing here is wrong—" / "—then everything at home
   is." / "...Get some sleep." Neither does. The town's bell keeps its schedule all
   night, and the dark around Farside Landing is full of lights that are not lamps,
   pulsing slow, and nobody watching them can decide, anymore, which side of the
   window the neighbor is on. End.

## Character tracking

- **Iyo**: "the sister's lot" + the posting — her rescue narrative is now officially
  in ruins; what replaces it (witness; negotiation; the ch. 23 ask) begins building
  here.
- **Cistern**: deliberately *held back* this chapter — he moves through the town in a
  gathering, luminous quiet (his interiority reserved for ch. 20, where it detonates).
  One beat only: a returned elder recognizes his gasket-rosary craft and shows him the
  town's chapel-shed. What he sees there is not described. He comes out changed. Hold.
- **Frame**: writ formally suspended by her own hand (paper trail); the warden is now
  a pilgrim with a sidearm.
- **Vell**: asks the "otherwise" question — still the man who reads terms and
  conditions; the Reeve's answer goes in his notes verbatim, underlined.
- **Ghost**: home; recedes to texture (corner seat, kibitzing on the chalk-game).
  His ch. 24 map-gift is his last function.
- **Elo**: notably *less* present in the town's frame — the Column's uncanny valley
  flattens here; among the returned he is unremarkable, and the Column keeps catching
  itself forgetting which one he is. (Stated once. Horrid.)

## The dread beat

"Nobody's missing. We know where everyone is." — the kindest sentence in the book and
its deepest destabilization: home's entire civilization is organized around a wound
Farside simply does not have. One of these towns is doing grief wrong, and the Column
can no longer tell which.

## Open questions

- The chalk-game's "counted back" rule: too pointed next to Iyo's rote? (It's the
  town's children casually holding the book's central mechanism as a *game* — that's
  the thesis; keep, but never let any adult explain it.)
- The chapel-shed: what did Cistern see? (Deliberately unwritten even in these notes
  until the ch. 20 refinement pass — whatever it is must work offstage forever;
  strong candidate: nothing but a maintained room and the town's version of the Hum,
  and the absence of any doctrine at all. The undoing of Cistern should be that it's
  *enough*.)
- "The sister's lot": confirm cold-readers don't parse it as prophecy/surveillance
  (intent: Farside has known about the Column longer than the Column has been
  walking — via the returned, via the ship; this is *implied infrastructure*, not
  omniscience, and must stay unglossed per guardrails).

### Game content pack

**Locations**: the quays (arrival stage; the chalk-post), the Vein-house (great hall —
doubles for ch. 20's Warming), billets, the net-sheds, the terraces (town roamable — hub
scale comparable to Saltcamp, register inverted: nothing is for sale), the chapel-shed

design rule that makes the town work).

**Side quests / optional content**:
- *The Tally-Game* — learn it from the children (the playable minigame from the game-notes
  doc lands here): turns of being It that end when you're "counted back." Charming by
  strict order of the design bible. Adults never explain it; asked, a child says "it's how
  you practice" and resumes.
- *Net Duty* — work a reef-line shift with the quay-crew (labor as hospitality-repayment;
  the town's only "currency"); one shift beside a returned crewmate — the game's returner
  grammar ambient at shoulder distance for a full scene.
- *The Census That Isn't* — Vell, professionally unable not to, starts a Farside census;
  the town finds this hilarious and cooperates wrongly (everyone answers for someone
  else); his abandoned worksheet is a paper-trail gem ("population: kept. see note.").
- *Iyo's Scan* — systemized quietly: every face in town can be looked at (the game logs
  nothing visibly; Maren is not here; players who check every face get one extra breath
  of Iyo VO at the net-shed ask — "she's not here" arriving pre-broken).

**Ambient events & barks**: the town bell's own schedule (never explained, kept by all);
returners taking the deep watches on the quays ("so the others don't have to" — Elo's
sentence, town-scale, unremarked); dogs here cross shadows they wouldn't cross at home;
the not-lamp lights at render distance, always, never approachable (locked law).

**Items, tokens & documents**: broth and bread (heat items, freely given — the economy
system reading zero-cost and the player feeling the wrongness of that), Vell's census
worksheet (paper trail), Frame's writ-suspension addendum (paper trail, from treatment).

**Audio priorities**: the town's room tone = home's (bells, latch ripples, kettle noise) —
the mix should be Hearthfall's with the Hum absent and reef-water present; the returned
given NO audio treatment whatsoever (the restraint IS the treatment); the tally-game
chant (the ch. 2 playground rhyme's cousin — same meter, different words; do not flag).
