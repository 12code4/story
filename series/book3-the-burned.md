# Book Three: The Burned — Plan

**One line**: The light is going out, and the night didn't do it — a mixed column of Verge,
woken, discharged, and Farside crosses the Glare, the hemisphere where nothing was ever
supposed to live, and finds the day side keeps things too.

**Thesis**: Book 1 taught the reader the dark's grammar (it asks). Book 3's engine is meeting
a presence with the *opposite* grammar — and discovering that a neighbor who never asks is
more terrifying than one who does. The survival story inverts whole: heat is the enemy, shade
is life, the lamps of Book 1 become parasols and smoked glass, and the mindfuck flips from
too-little-seeing to **too much** — mirage, doubling, a world of light that shows you more
than is there.

**Timeline**: opens weeks after Book 2's muster (Year 184). One crossing, one year.

## The engine (what's known at open, per Book 2)

The Ember's measured output is unchanged; the light *arriving* thins anyway, now visibly
(the flicker). The night's proposal: cooperation — guides of its own kind cannot cross the
terminator (parked: why not), but its knowledge, its provisioning, and its people (the
discharged, the returned) can. The expedition is humanity's first act as the *senior partner*
in a covenant: the dark is trusting them with an errand it cannot run. Nobody says that
sentence aloud. Everybody knows it.

## The column (working roster)

- **Iyo** — commanding, formally now: Warrant of the Line (the title invented for her; Kiln's
  ghost in every use of it). **Maren** beside her — the sisters' expedition: one warm, one
  cold-handed, one line. The book's heart is finishing what Book 2 started: two people
  relearning sisterhood *as colleagues* on the worst road there is.
- **Brakes + Tapper** — the shade-crawler *Noon* (her second masterpiece; Kettle's ghost in
  every joke she doesn't make). Tapper's chassis re-skinned for heat; the woken automatists'
  upgrades make it partly *their* work — Tapper as the first joint artifact of the two
  cultures.
- **Solene** — chief listener; in the Glare the band does the opposite of the Sea: it
  *overfills* (light-noise, phantom carriers, her own transmissions arriving before she
  sends them — the mindfuck inverted; discontinuities of surplus).
- **Ilva Cade** — the woken's delegate and the reader's outsider-eyes again; also the book's
  botanist: things *grow* in the Glare's twilight edge. Wrong things. Beautifully.
- **New: a Farside walker-guide** (the returned can't cross; a living Stayer volunteers —
  Cale Stay, grown), and **Captain Sorn's** second, **Edri Voss**, a woken surveyor who
  carries the Meridian-era survey charts of Taper — the ones the Errand diverted against.
  (The diversion's target is on them: a marked site on the day side. Book 3 goes there.
  Why the Errand was aimed there in the first place: purchased late, at cost.)
- **Adder** — declines the muster. Stays, trains, keeps the line. His arc is complete;
  the book lets a soldier be done. (One letter from him in the paper trail, worth the
  whole cast's dialogue.)
- **Elo** — at the terminator's edge, his function ends: "I don't cross." The Column walks
  out of the reach of the only grammar it has learned to trust. (He is the book's last
  image too — see ending.)

## Part structure (five parts, key beats)

1. **The Sun-Line (act 1)** — the Verge's other edge, where no line was ever kept: building
   the first sun-line posts; the Glare kit (shade-discipline, smoked glass, the *Noon*);
   the departure through heat-shimmer instead of dark. First inversion beats: cindermoths
   will not cross it either; dogs scream at the *day*.
2. **The Ashlands (act 2)** — the near Glare: burned relics of the Errand's original
   survey drops (pre-crash hardware, Meridian-marked — Voss's charts coming alive);
   travel by shade-hop between rock shadows; heat as the draining resource; the first
   sign that the thinning is *local to something*: shadows in the Ashlands fall slightly
   wrong, toward a common point. Stated once.
3. **The Mirror Sea (act 3)** — salt flats under permanent noon: the mindfuck's home
   ground. Mirage discipline (what you see must be voted on — the Ghost problem
   inverted and weaponized by terrain); light-fauna (things that live *in* image — the
   Benthos' counterpart, glimpsed as reflections that persist after the object moves);
   the storm equivalent: white-outs of *excess seeing* after which the discontinuities
   are additions (extra objects, extra tracks, one extra tent that no one will enter).
   Midpoint cost lands here (candidate: the *Noon* — Brakes loses her second great
   machine to the light as she lost her first to the water; or a legacy-cast death —
   decide at the book's own gate; the series should not repeat the Lead's shape
   beat-for-beat).
4. **The Stillpoint (act 4)** — the subsolar country; the source. What keeps the day
   (two candidate manners, parked, to be decided with the truth braid): (a) a presence
   that never asks and only gives — light, heat, growth, more than can be survived,
   generosity as predation, the anti-grammar of the night's courtesy; or (b) an
   artifact/engine — something *built*, older than the colony, drinking the Ember's
   output for a purpose not yet legible (ties to why the Errand was aimed here).
   Either way: the diversion's target found; the Second Landing's fate answered here
   (they didn't vanish nightward — the records were wrong by one word; purchased
   answer, arrives sideways, costs); and the errand performed — the thinning addressed
   at a price that must be paid *by someone staying* (the book's Cistern-shaped hole:
   candidate — Cale Stay keeps the Stillpoint as Farside kept the night; or Brakes,
   the keeper-arc's terminal temptation, REFUSED this time by her own spoken no —
   test both at drafting).
5. **The Return by Twilight (act 5)** — home along the terminator itself, the knife-edge
   road between the two neighbors; the world triaged: two borders now, two lines, one
   people between. Coda target: Iyo at the sun-line's first finished post, Maren beside
   her; the light measurably recovering; and from the dark behind them, at line-distance,
   Elo — facing the *day* now — raising his lamp to a sun he cannot approach. The series'
   geometry: three-body. End of Book Three.

## Costs (provisional, series-balanced)

Book 3 is the second-most lethal after Book 1. Reserved candidates: the *Noon*; one of
(Cale Stay / Voss); the Stillpoint's keeper (whoever stays); and — held card — **Maren**:
the braid where the day takes what the night returned is the series' cruelest available
move; decide only at Book 3's gate, with Book 4's ending options on the table. (If Maren
survives Book 3, Book 4 belongs to the sisters; if not, Book 4 belongs to the count.)

## Game notes

The inverted survival game: shade as the master resource (the flame meter's mirror — a
shadow meter), heat-stroke replacing frost on the party strip, travel as shade-hopping
route-puzzles, mirage-voting as a party mechanic (the crew must agree what's real before
the map accepts it), the *Noon* as mobile shade (Kettle's mirror). The radio inverts:
tuning *out* instead of in. New-player entry point: Book 3 is designed to be playable
first (the crossing structure re-teaches everything), with Book 1–2 saves imported for
door/roster continuity.

## Guardrail check

The day's presence gets the Book-1 treatment from scratch: manner before nature, no
explainer, one big overt impossibility spent at midpoint, rules kept privately. The
misdirection (light-thinning ≠ the night) resolves as *relief that curdles* — the
neighbor you feared is the one who warned you. Answers purchased this book: the
diversion's target, the Second Landing, the thinning's mechanism. Questions opened:
what the Stillpoint wants, what the terminator is *for*, and why the night cannot cross
— each bigger than what was paid for.
