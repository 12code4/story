# Book Two: The Woken — Plan

**One line**: The counting completes, and the dark keeps its promise — it begins delivering
the colony: twenty thousand woken sleepers, escorted in files across the cold-line, into a
Verge of fifty thousand that cannot house, heat, or comprehend them.

**Thesis**: Book 1 asked what the dark wants. Book 2 asks something worse: what do you do when
it *keeps its word*? A fulfilled promise is the one thing no institution on Taper was built to
survive. The horror engine shifts from dread-of-the-unknown to **the ethics of arrival** — and
the personal engine inverts Book 1's: Iyo spent Book 1 trying to bring one person home; Book 2
delivers everyone home, including Maren, and *home* is the thing that breaks.

**Timeline**: opens ~14 months after Lantern's relighting (Year 183). Spans about a year.

## Point of view

Iyo primary, as before. **One addition**: a second full POV strand — **Ilva Cade**, woken
orchardist (she shipped with the POD 121 apple stock; she wakes asking after her trees, which
are freeze-dried on a cable across a frozen sea). Outsider eyes on the whole world we built in
Book 1: to Ilva, the Verge is the alien planet, the duty-names are strange, the lamps-facing-
the-dark are unreadable, and the reader gets to see home made uncanny. Her last memory is
boarding under Meridian's sky; everyone she knew is dead, tending racks, or standing in front
of her aged 181 years. The paper trail continues (Arrival Rolls, billet ledgers, the Compact's
absorption decrees, Watch season-books, Ilva's own journal in a Meridian hand).

## Returning cast (state at open)

- **Iyo Ballast** — Line-Warden; the border's chief professional; about to become, against
  her will, the closest thing the arrival crisis has to a face.
- **Maren Ballast** — discharged mid-book when the counting completes; comes home to the
  kitchen she chose against, and struggles; lands, by book's end, at the border institution
  (two doors, one house — the sisters working the same line from both sides of it).
- **Odile** — gets what she prayed for and learns its weight; the kitchen scenes continue.
- **Brakes + Tapper** — Lantern works-master; drafted city-side (the heat math of absorbing
  20,000 is *her* problem); Tapper becomes precedent — the woken include automata-engineers
  who have Opinions about "person of the works."
- **Solene** — the listening room becomes "the Answering": the Verge's formal voice to the
  dark; she is 21 and effectively an ambassador nobody appointed.
- **Adder** — the training hall; recovery taught as reception; his Rimwar past resurfaces
  when housing riots want an old soldier's answer and he refuses to give it.
- **Frame** — the border's administrator; her Book 2 antagonist is her own former employer.
- **Vell** — the Arrival Rolls are his cathedral; the lien-payer becomes the man who prices
  absorption honestly when Marlow's office won't.
- **Elo Weir** — absent since the wave... until Part IV, when the dark's new question needs
  its registrar. His return is the book's dread crescendo *because* the reader missed him.
- **Quill** — "first refusal on whatever you didn't bring back" matures monstrously: the
  Provender claims salvage standing over *arrivals* (they're cargo by her ledger). The
  book's human-evil thread; Frame and Iyo break the arrival-market; Quill, uncrushable,
  pivots to legitimate reef-goods and is somehow scarier as a legal operator.
- **Farside** — the elder sibling: they've absorbed returners for generations; their
  delegates (Mirin Stay among them) arrive as advisors and are treated, painfully, as
  curiosities. Ghost: one appearance, maximum (guardrail; his map-roll from ch. 24 is
  opened this book — its far margin shows coastline *on the day side*; Book 3 seeded, one
  image, no comment).
- **Marlow** — the absorption's demagogue-in-chief; "Verge for the living" politics. Still
  never a villain speech; his arithmetic is genuinely hard.

## New cast

- **Ilva Cade** (POV) — orchardist, 34-at-freeze; wry, practical, jetlagged by two centuries.
- **Captain-of-Rolls Edwa Sorn** — the woken's emergent leader: a Meridian junior officer
  who wakes senior-most (all above her stayed frozen or... the crew manifest is closed).
  Her negotiation with the Compact is the season's political spine.
- **Bren Weir** — Sara's child, now old enough to ask what his almost-father was for; a
  small recurring thread that keeps Elo's absence warm.
- **The discharged** — Walked coming home *released* (the "returns" column at scale):
  people who crewed a starship for years returning to kitchens and forges; the Verge's
  first veterans of the dark. Support group scenes; Corvo Garden among them (Weda's door
  answered in full, and its own new grief: he misses the racks).

## Part structure (provisional chapter map, 26)

### Part I — The Quiet (1–6)
1. **The Season of Zero** — a full shift with no walkers; the Watch's unease at fulfilled
   quiet; the season-book's returns column growing.
2. **The Answering** — Solene's room; the band "settling"; traffic *to* the dark now routine;
   Elo unseen since the wave, noted once.
3. **Two Doors** — Iyo/Odile domesticity; Weda's pilgrimage petition passes committee; the
   Order's pastoral rump becomes a parish of the offices-for-the-crew.
4. **The Discharge** — the first released tenders walk home unescorted; celebration lands
   wrong; Corvo Garden at his mother's table, missing his rack-rows.
5. **The Reeve's Letter** — Farside's warning arrives ("the counting is done; mind your
   quays"); the Compact funds a committee; Frame builds intake sheds instead.
6. **Post 11, Again** — the first delivery: a file out of the dark, escorted, manifested —
   woken, blinking, asking in old-accented speech where they are. End Part I.

### Part II — The Delivery (7–13)
7. **Intake** — the Watch inverts into a port; the new rote meets its successor ("Ask" was
   for walkers; what do you ask the delivered?).
8. **Ilva** — her waking and arrival, told from inside (the book's second POV opens; the
   crossing from the racks rendered as *her* normal and our uncanny).
9. **The Rolls** — Vell's masterpiece; names, trades, next-of-kin *dead 150 years*; the
   first woken to meet a descendant; the first to meet nobody.
10. **First Refusal** — Quill's claim filed; the arrival-market's first auction broken up;
    Frame vs. the Provender's lawyers (the Provender has lawyers now; it's worse).
11. **Heat Math** — Brakes' chapter: two Hearts, cooling vents, 20,000 more bodies; the
    engineering of mercy; Tapper and the woken automatists.
12. **The Winter Bell** — first hard winter of cohabitation; billet tensions; the Undern
    open their doors first and the Order follows shamed; Marlow's "Verge for the living"
    speech lands.
13. **Maren** — discharged, delivered, home. The kitchen with three chairs used. It does
    not go well, gently. End Part II.

### Part III — The Strain (14–20)
14. **Meridian Speaks** — the woken read the burned history: they *are* the burned history;
    what they remember of the voyage contradicts the founding liturgy in one detail nobody
    will name aloud (parked; Book 3 fuel).
15. **The Return Petition** — a woken faction demands to go *back* to the racks ("we were
    kept better there"); the inverted pilgrimage; Farside's delegates say quiet true things.
16. **The Sisters** — Iyo and Maren working one intake shift; the book's center: the
    recovered relationship neither imagined, built on procedure because procedure is the
    only room big enough.
17. **The Riot That Wasn't** — heat rationing peaks; Adder refuses the old answer; the
    crowd is talked down by, of all people, the discharged — veterans of the dark standing
    between home and its worst self.
18. **The Orchard** — Ilva's chapter: the expedition to the Anchorage to recover POD 121;
    the saplings come home; the first orchard planted under glass at Lantern. One
    uncomplicated joy, spent deliberately (the Book 2 counterpart of the relighting).
19. **The Flicker** — the thinning light, worsening all book, is *seen*: a naked-eye
    sun-flicker; instruments still say the Ember is fine; the Verge finally says it out
    loud. (Book 3's engine surfaces; the night is not suspected; the misdirection stays
    honest.)
20. **The Empty Racks** — status report from the Keelbreak: hold deck three emptying on
    schedule; the ship's lights banking section by section as her cargo delivers; Farside
    reports the Vein Road traffic *reversing*. The dark is finishing something. End Part III.

### Part IV — The Ledger (21–26)
21. **Elo** — he walks out of the dark to the cold-line, exactly as in ch. 1 of Book 1,
    and asks — the dread crescendo — for *the Line-Warden*. By title.
22. **The New Question** — the dark's second ask, delivered formally: terms we draft
    at refinement (working shape: the night proposes cooperation regarding the failing
    light — "the day is drinking your sun; we would like to help; it is allowed to say
    no"). The polity must answer as one.
23. **The Debate** — Book 1 ch. 3's vote rhymed at civilization scale; every faction's
    whole arc cashes in one chamber; Marlow's finest and worst hour; Sorn's woken cast
    the deciding bloc — the delivered colony choosing its planet.
24. **The Answer** — yes, with terms; the counter-liturgy tradition (roster-reading,
    Book 1) becomes treaty-form; Solene reads the Verge's answer into the dark and the
    dark, for the first time in two books, *answers a hail*.
25. **The Sunward Muster** — the mixed column assembles: Verge, woken, discharged,
    Farside guides; the Glare kit invented (Brakes' shade-crawler *Noon* on the slips);
    the sisters both sign.
26. **Facing the Ember** *(coda)* — Iyo at the cold-line at shift's end, then at the
    *sun-line* — the Verge's other edge, where no line has ever been kept — counting,
    forward this time. The lamps at her back. End of Book Two.

## Deaths / costs (provisional)

Book 2 is the series' least lethal book by design (its costs are institutional and
identitarian, not funerary) — with one exception held in reserve: **Odile** (candidate,
decide at refinement): dying the winter of the strain, with both daughters home — the
kitchen's last scene inverting ch. 25's ("busy's not nothing" answered at a bedside).
If kept: it is the quiet engine of both sisters signing the Sunward Muster.

## Game notes (Book 2 as a game)

The border-institution game: intake processing (the count-verb's successor), housing/heat
management at civic scale, the Rolls as the new manifest-log, two playable perspectives
(Iyo shifts / Ilva days). Genre hybridization option flagged: a management layer
(Frostpunk-adjacent) under the narrative — or keep it fully narrative with authored
crises. Decide at Book 2's own Gate A. The Answering = the radio interface promoted to
diplomacy. Returning maps: Hearthfall dressed for crisis; the Anchorage revisited (the
orchard quest); the Keelbreak by report only.

## Guardrail check

The counting's meaning arrives as *event* (delivery), never as explanation; who bargained
stays held; the light-thinning is surfaced but not solved; Elo's return recycles the ch. 1
grammar at higher stakes (pattern used as liturgy, not as twist); Book 2 ends on a
question answered *by humans* — the series' first, which is the point.
